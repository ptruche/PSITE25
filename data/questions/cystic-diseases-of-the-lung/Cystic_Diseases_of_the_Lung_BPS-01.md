A 3-month-old infant is evaluated for recurrent left lower lobe infections. CT angiography reveals a mass in the posterior basal segment supplied by an anomalous artery from the thoracic aorta and draining into the left inferior pulmonary vein. Which of the following statements best characterizes this lesion?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

**Bronchopulmonary sequestration (BPS)** is a **congenital malformation of nonfunctioning lung tissue** that lacks communication with the normal tracheobronchial tree and receives **systemic arterial blood supply**, typically from the **thoracic or abdominal aorta**. This abnormal vascular pattern distinguishes it from other cystic lung lesions such as **congenital pulmonary airway malformation (CPAM)**.

---

### **1) Defining Features of BPS**
- **No communication** with native airways (“sequestered” from the bronchial tree).  
- **Systemic arterial supply**, usually from the **descending thoracic or upper abdominal aorta**.  
- **Venous drainage** differs by type:  
  - **Intralobar BPS:** drains into **pulmonary veins**.  
  - **Extralobar BPS:** drains into **systemic veins** (e.g., azygos, hemiazygos, or portal veins).  
- Both types consist of **nonfunctioning lung tissue** that does not participate in gas exchange.  

| **Feature** | **Intralobar BPS** | **Extralobar BPS** |
|--------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **Pleural covering** | Shared with normal lung | Separate pleura |
| **Arterial supply** | Systemic (aorta) | Systemic (aorta) |
| **Venous drainage** | Pulmonary veins | Systemic veins |
| **Airway communication** | Absent | Absent |
| **Infection risk** | High (shares pleura with native lung) | Low |
| **Location** | Within thorax | Thoracic or subdiaphragmatic |

---

### **2) Pathophysiology and Hemodynamics**
Because the sequestered tissue is supplied by systemic arteries, **large BPS lesions can shunt blood away from systemic circulation**, leading to:  
- **High-output cardiac failure** due to “steal” phenomenon.  
- **Cardiomegaly** and volume overload in severe cases.  
- This risk is highest in **large intralobar lesions** with high-flow systemic supply.

---

### **3) Intralobar vs. Extralobar Variants**
**Intralobar BPS (≈75% of cases):**
- Lies **within the visceral pleura** of the normal lung.  
- Shares pleural investment and pulmonary venous drainage.  
- Often **presents later in childhood** with **recurrent pneumonia or infection** due to proximity to infected airways.  
- Commonly found in **posterior basal segments of the lower lobes**.

**Extralobar BPS (≈25% of cases):**
- Has its **own pleural covering**, separate from the normal lung.  
- Venous drainage is **systemic** (azygos, hemiazygos, or portal veins).  
- Usually **detected prenatally or in infancy**.  
- **Infections are rare** because the lesion is isolated from the aerated lung.  
- May occur **below the diaphragm** (subdiaphragmatic sequestration).

| **Characteristic** | **Intralobar** | **Extralobar** |
|----------------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Prevalence** | 75% | 25% |
| **Age at presentation** | Childhood / adolescence | Infancy / prenatal |
| **Drainage** | Pulmonary veins | Systemic veins |
| **Infection risk** | High | Low |
| **Associated anomalies** | Rare | Common (e.g., diaphragmatic hernia) |

---

### **4) Relationship to CPAM and Hybrid Lesions**
While BPS and CPAM are distinct entities, **hybrid lesions** can contain features of both:  
- **Systemic arterial supply (BPS component)** and **bronchial communication (CPAM component)**.  
- These hybrid lesions emphasize the **shared embryologic origin** of congenital lung malformations.

| **Feature** | **CPAM** | **BPS** |
|--------------|-----------|----------|
| **Airway communication** | Present | Absent |
| **Arterial supply** | Pulmonary | Systemic |
| **Drainage** | Pulmonary veins | Pulmonary or systemic veins |
| **Infection risk** | Moderate | High (intralobar) |
| **Hybrid lesions** | Common | Common |

---

### **5) Clinical Presentation and Management**
- **Symptoms:** recurrent pneumonia, cough, failure to thrive, or incidental prenatal detection.  
- **Diagnosis:** CT or MR angiography showing systemic arterial supply.  
- **Treatment:** surgical resection (lobectomy or segmentectomy) to prevent infection or heart failure.  
- **Prognosis:** excellent after complete excision.

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** BPS does *not* communicate with the bronchial tree and is supplied by systemic, not pulmonary, arteries.  
- **B:** Extralobar BPS drains to systemic veins and rarely becomes infected.  
- **C:** Intralobar BPS is within shared pleura, not separate.  
- **E:** Both variants are supplied by systemic, not pulmonary, arteries.  

**Correct answer: D — Bronchopulmonary sequestration is defined by the absence of airway communication and a systemic arterial blood supply, with intralobar lesions draining via pulmonary veins and extralobar lesions via systemic veins.**
