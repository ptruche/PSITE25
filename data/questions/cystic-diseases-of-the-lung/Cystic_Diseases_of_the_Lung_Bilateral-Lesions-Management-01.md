A 5-month-old infant presents with bilateral congenital pulmonary airway malformations (CPAMs), with an intralobar lesion on one side and an extralobar sequestration on the other. The infant is currently stable. Which of the following statements best reflects current surgical management principles?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

### **Management of Bilateral Congenital Lung Lesions**

Bilateral congenital lung malformations—such as **CPAMs** and **bronchopulmonary sequestrations (BPS)**—are rare. Management must balance adequate resection with preservation of functional lung tissue to avoid long-term respiratory compromise.

---

### **1) Surgical Strategy: Staged vs. Simultaneous Resection**

| **Approach** | **Indication / Rationale** |
|---------------|-----------------------------|
| **Staged Resection** | Allows for recovery after the first operation, especially after lobectomy; useful when significant resection is expected on both sides. |
| **Simultaneous Resection** | Appropriate for stable patients; reduces total anesthesia exposure and hospitalization; may be necessary when a **systemic feeding vessel crosses the mediastinum**, since unilateral ligation could lead to **necrosis on the opposite side**. |

- Decision between staged vs simultaneous surgery is **based on patient stability, lesion extent, and vascular anatomy**.  

---

### **2) Surgical Technique**

- **Lung-preserving surgery** (segmentectomy or partial lobectomy) is favored whenever possible, to maintain maximum functional lung tissue.  
- Lesions may be **intralobar on one side** and **extralobar on the other**; each should be managed according to its own anatomic characteristics.  
- Both **open** and **thoracoscopic** approaches are acceptable, depending on surgeon experience and patient physiology.  

---

### **3) Evidence from Pediatric Series**

| **Condition Studied** | **Findings** |
|------------------------|--------------|
| **Hydatid disease (bilateral thoracotomy)** | Staged resections had few complications, ~8-day hospital stay. |
| **Pediatric metastasectomy** | Simultaneous resections reduced ICU and hospital length of stay without increasing morbidity. |

These data suggest that **simultaneous procedures** can be safe and efficient in selected stable pediatric patients.  

---

### **4) Anesthetic and Technical Considerations**

- **Simultaneous procedures** require intraoperative repositioning and endotracheal tube adjustments.  
- If **bilateral lobectomy** is required, a **staged approach** is safer to allow recovery from prior resection and minimize pulmonary complications (e.g. persistent pneumothorax).  

---

### **5) Key Principles**

- **Either staged or simultaneous surgery** may be appropriate depending on patient condition.  
- **Lung-preserving resections** should be prioritized in all bilateral lesions.  
- **Cross-mediastinal feeding vessels** favor same-day bilateral surgery.  
- **Multidisciplinary planning** with anesthesia and critical care is essential for optimizing safety and outcomes.  

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** Staged procedures are not mandatory; simultaneous surgery is acceptable in select stable cases.  
- **B:** Bilateral surgery is safe when carefully planned.  
- **D:** Simultaneous surgery is not required for all patients.  
- **E:** Extralobar sequestrations should be resected when identified, not observed indefinitely.  

**Correct answer: C — Bilateral congenital lung lesions can be approached in either a staged or simultaneous fashion depending on clinical stability, with lung-preserving surgery preferred.**
