A 4-month-old infant is found to have a mediastinal cystic lesion on prenatal imaging, confirmed postnatally to be a bronchogenic cyst. The child is currently asymptomatic. What is the recommended management?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

### **Bronchogenic Cysts: Pathophysiology and Management**

**Bronchogenic cysts** are congenital, unilocular cystic lesions that arise from **abnormal budding of the primitive foregut** during embryologic development. They are most commonly located in the **mediastinum (paratracheal or subcarinal)** and lined by **ciliated, mucus-secreting columnar epithelium**.

---

### **1) Complications**

Although many bronchogenic cysts are initially **asymptomatic**, they carry significant risks, including:

| **Complication** | **Mechanism / Effect** |
|------------------|------------------------|
| **Infection** | Bacterial contamination via airway communication |
| **Mass effect** | Compression of trachea, esophagus, or great vessels (may cause **dysphagia or respiratory distress**) |
| **Hemoptysis** | From erosion into bronchial structures |
| **Pneumothorax** | From cyst rupture into pleural space |
| **Malignant degeneration** | Rare, but reported transformation to carcinoma |

Due to these risks, **surgical excision** is recommended for all identified cysts.

---

### **2) Diagnostic Evaluation**

- **Multidetector CT with Angiography (MDCTA)** is the imaging modality of choice.  
  - Confirms cystic nature and excludes alternative diagnoses (e.g., CPAM or duplication cyst).  
  - Defines anatomical relationships and guides operative planning.  
- MRI may be considered if iodinated contrast is contraindicated.  

---

### **3) Timing of Surgery**

| **Scenario** | **Management** |
|---------------|----------------|
| **Prenatally diagnosed, asymptomatic** | Elective resection at **3–6 months** of age |
| **Symptomatic or infected** | Stabilize (antibiotics ± aspiration) → **definitive resection when stable** |

- Early surgery allows for safe anesthesia and avoids complications such as infection or compression.  
- Delay beyond infancy is not recommended due to cumulative risk of complications.  

---

### **4) Operative Approach**

- **Thoracoscopic resection** is preferred in centers with expertise; open thoracotomy remains a safe alternative.  
- Complete excision prevents recurrence and allows histologic confirmation.  

---

### **5) Key Principles**

- **All bronchogenic cysts warrant surgical resection.**  
- **CT with angiography** is essential for diagnostic confirmation and surgical planning.  
- **Delay in resection** increases the likelihood of infection, compression, or malignant change.
