A 4-month-old infant presents with progressive stridor and recurrent respiratory infections. Chest CT demonstrates a well-circumscribed, fluid-filled mass in the subcarinal region compressing the main bronchi. Which of the following best characterizes this lesion?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

**Bronchogenic cysts** are **foregut-derived developmental anomalies** that form as **unilocular, mucus-filled cysts** arising from abnormal budding of the tracheobronchial tree during embryogenesis. They are among the most common **mediastinal cystic lesions in children** and may present with airway obstruction or infection.

---

### **1) Embryologic Origin and Pathology**
- Originates from **abnormal budding of the ventral foregut** between **weeks 3–7 of gestation**.  
- Represents a **duplication of tracheobronchial tissue** that detaches from the developing airway.  
- The cyst wall contains **cartilaginous, smooth muscle, and mucous gland elements**, resembling normal bronchial tissue.  
- **Epithelium:** ciliated, mucus-secreting, pseudostratified columnar lining.  
- **Contents:** thick, mucinous material.  
- **Unilocular**, differentiating them from the multicystic appearance of CPAMs.

| **Histologic Feature** | **Description** |
|--------------------------|-----------------|
| **Epithelium** | Ciliated columnar, mucus-secreting |
| **Wall components** | Cartilage, smooth muscle, glands |
| **Contents** | Mucinous fluid |
| **Inflammation** | Common with infection |

---

### **2) Location and Radiologic Features**
- Most commonly found in the **mediastinum**, particularly:  
  - **Paratracheal region**  
  - **Subcarinal region**  
  - **Hilar or peribronchial locations**  
- Less commonly located **within lung parenchyma** (intrapulmonary bronchogenic cyst).  
- Because of their **central location**, they can cause **compression of the airway** or **esophagus**, leading to:  
  - Stridor, cough, recurrent pneumonia, dysphagia, or respiratory distress.  
  - **Distal lung hyperinflation** due to air trapping.

| **Feature** | **Bronchogenic Cyst** | **CPAM** |
|--------------|----------------------|-----------|
| **Location** | Mediastinal (central) | Peripheral (usually lower lobe) |
| **Number** | Unilocular | Multilocular / cystic |
| **Airway communication** | None | Present |
| **Epithelium** | Ciliated, mucus-secreting | Pseudostratified, nonsecretory |
| **Cartilage** | Present | Rare |
| **Vascular supply** | Pulmonary | Pulmonary |

---

### **3) Clinical Presentation**
- **Asymptomatic:** may be incidentally discovered on imaging.  
- **Symptomatic:** result from compression of adjacent structures.  
  - **Airway compression:** stridor, wheezing, dyspnea.  
  - **Esophageal compression:** dysphagia or feeding difficulty.  
  - **Infection:** cysts can become secondarily infected or form abscesses.  
  - **Expansion:** mucin accumulation leads to progressive enlargement.

---

### **4) Complications**
- **Airway obstruction** and respiratory distress (especially in infants).  
- **Recurrent infections** or **abscess formation**.  
- Rarely, **malignant transformation** has been reported in adults.  
- **Cyst rupture** may lead to pneumothorax or empyema.

---

### **5) Management and Prognosis**
- **Diagnosis:** confirmed by CT or MRI; shows a well-circumscribed, fluid-density lesion without enhancement.  
- **Definitive treatment:** **surgical excision**, even in asymptomatic cases, to prevent infection or compression.  
- **Prognosis:** excellent after resection.

---

**Key distinguishing features:**  
- **Central mediastinal location.**  
- **Ciliated, mucus-secreting epithelium with cartilage.**  
- **Unilocular cystic lesion.**  
- **May compress adjacent airways or esophagus.**  
- **Prone to infection and enlargement.**

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** CPAMs are peripheral and multicystic, not central or unilocular.  
- **C:** Systemic arterial supply occurs in sequestrations, not bronchogenic cysts.  
- **D:** The cyst is lined by ciliated columnar, not squamous, epithelium.  
- **E:** CLE involves overinflation, not cyst formation.  

**Correct answer: B — Bronchogenic cysts are centrally located, unilocular mediastinal cysts lined by ciliated mucus-secreting columnar epithelium and may cause airway compression or infection.**
