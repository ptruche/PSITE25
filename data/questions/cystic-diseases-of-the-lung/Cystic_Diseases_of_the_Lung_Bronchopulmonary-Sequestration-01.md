A 2-month-old infant is found to have a left lower lobe lesion consistent with bronchopulmonary sequestration on prenatal imaging. The infant is asymptomatic. What is the most appropriate management strategy?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

### **Bronchopulmonary Sequestration (BPS): Management and Surgical Principles**

**Bronchopulmonary sequestration (BPS)** is a congenital pulmonary malformation characterized by **non-functioning lung tissue** that lacks communication with the tracheobronchial tree and receives **systemic arterial blood supply**, typically from the **thoracic or abdominal aorta**.

---

### **1) Types of Sequestration**

| **Type** | **Pleural Investment** | **Venous Drainage** | **Infection Risk** | **Location** |
|-----------|------------------------|----------------------|--------------------|----------------|
| **Intralobar (ILS)** | Within visceral pleura of normal lung | Pulmonary veins | High – air enters via pores of Kohn | Typically lower lobes |
| **Extralobar (ELS)** | Separate pleural investment | Systemic veins | Low – isolated from airway | May occur below diaphragm |

---

### **2) Diagnostic Workup**

- **Multidetector CT with Angiography (MDCTA)** is **essential** for diagnosis and surgical planning.  
  - Defines **systemic arterial supply** and **venous drainage pattern**.  
  - Identifies **subdiaphragmatic or intrathoracic ELS**.  
- **Esophagram** should be considered for **subdiaphragmatic lesions** to differentiate from **esophageal duplication cysts**.  

---

### **3) Management Approach**

| **Clinical Scenario** | **Recommended Strategy** | **Rationale** |
|------------------------|--------------------------|----------------|
| **Intralobar sequestration (ILS)** | **Elective resection** | High risk of infection due to collateral ventilation via pores of Kohn |
| **Extralobar sequestration (ELS)** | **Observation** for small, cyst-free lesions with minimal systemic supply | Low infection risk and potential for spontaneous regression |
| **Symptomatic lesions** | **Surgical resection** (thoracoscopic or open) | Prevents infection and cardiac overload |

---

### **4) Timing of Surgery**

- **ILS:** Timing similar to **CPAM**, ideally before **6–12 months of age** to minimize risk of infection and allow for compensatory lung growth.  
- **ELS:** Elective surgery if symptomatic or enlarging; observation reasonable in small, isolated lesions.  

---

### **5) Operative Planning**

- **Preoperative imaging** is critical due to variable lesion location (thoracic, diaphragmatic, or subdiaphragmatic).  
- **Systemic feeding vessel** must be precisely identified and ligated to avoid hemorrhage.  
- **Thoracoscopic approach** is feasible in stable infants with clear anatomy.  

---

### **6) Key Points**

- **ILS → resect**, **ELS → may observe** if small and uncomplicated.  
- **MDCTA** is mandatory for diagnosis and surgical mapping.  
- **Esophagram** helps rule out esophageal duplication in subdiaphragmatic cases.  
- Surgery before 1 year of age reduces complications and promotes lung development.  

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** Not all ELS require urgent resection; small cyst-free lesions can be observed.  
- **B:** ILS carries significant infection risk, requiring resection.  
- **C:** CT angiography is essential for surgical planning.

**Correct answer: D — In asymptomatic patients, resection of ILS is recommended due to infection risk, while small, cyst-free ELS lesions with minimal blood supply may be safely observed.**
