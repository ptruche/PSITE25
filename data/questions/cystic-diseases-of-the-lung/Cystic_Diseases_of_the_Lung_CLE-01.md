A 2-week-old infant presents with progressive respiratory distress. Chest radiograph demonstrates hyperlucency of the left upper lobe with mediastinal shift. CT confirms overexpansion of a single lobe without cyst formation or systemic vascular supply. Which of the following statements best characterizes this condition?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

**Congenital lobar emphysema (CLE)**, also referred to as **congenital lobar overinflation**, is a **developmental pulmonary disorder** characterized by overdistention of a pulmonary lobe due to defective bronchial cartilage, mucosal folds, or extrinsic airway compression. Unlike acquired emphysema, CLE **does not involve alveolar wall destruction**.

---

### **1) Pathophysiology and Terminology**
- The term *“congenital lobar emphysema”* is a **misnomer**, as histologically there is **no alveolar destruction**—a hallmark of adult emphysema.  
- Instead, **air trapping during respiration** causes progressive **lobar overinflation** and mass effect.  
- The preferred modern term is **congenital lobar overinflation (CLO)**.  

| **Feature** | **CLE (CLO)** | **Acquired Emphysema** |
|--------------|---------------|------------------------|
| **Pathology** | Overinflation without alveolar destruction | Alveolar wall destruction |
| **Cause** | Developmental cartilage defect or bronchial obstruction | Cigarette smoke, elastase injury |
| **Age** | Neonatal / infant | Adult |
| **Treatment** | Lobectomy if symptomatic | Medical management (smoking cessation, bronchodilators) |

---

### **2) Anatomic Distribution**
- **Left upper lobe** is most commonly involved (≈50%), sometimes sparing the lingula.  
- **Right middle lobe** is the second most frequent site.  
- Rarely, segmental disease occurs and may be **asymptomatic**, detected incidentally on imaging.  
- **Bilateral CLE** is rare.  

| **Frequency by Lobe** | **Approximate Distribution** |
|------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Left upper lobe | 40–50% |
| Right middle lobe | 30% |
| Right upper / lower lobes | 10–20% |

---

### **3) Etiology**
Overinflation occurs secondary to:  
- **Intrinsic airway abnormality:** bronchial cartilage deficiency, mucosal redundancy, or bronchomalacia.  
- **Extrinsic compression:** vascular sling, bronchial atresia, or adjacent mass.  
- **Functional obstruction:** ball-valve mechanism from mucus plug or inflammation.  

In rare segmental cases, mild **bronchial ectasia** may be present, placing the lesion within the **bronchial atresia spectrum**.

---

### **4) Clinical Presentation and Diagnosis**
- **Neonatal presentation:** respiratory distress, tachypnea, or cyanosis within first weeks of life.  
- **Imaging findings:** hyperlucent, overinflated lobe with mediastinal shift and compression of adjacent lung.  
- **CT scan:** confirms absence of systemic arterial supply and lack of cystic structure (distinguishing it from CPAM or BPS).  
- **Differential diagnosis:** pneumothorax, CPAM, bronchial atresia.  

---

### **5) Management and Prognosis**
- **Asymptomatic cases:** observation, as some spontaneously improve.  
- **Symptomatic cases:** **lobectomy** is curative and well tolerated due to compensatory growth of remaining lung tissue.  
- **Prognosis:** excellent after surgical resection.  

---

### **6) Key Differentiating Features**
| **Feature** | **CLE** | **CPAM** | **BPS** |
|--------------|----------|----------|----------|
| **Airway communication** | Yes | Yes | No |
| **Arterial supply** | Pulmonary | Pulmonary | Systemic |
| **Cystic change** | None | Cystic | None or cystic |
| **Functional alveoli** | Present but overexpanded | Absent | Nonfunctional |
| **Common lobe** | LUL > RML | Lower lobes | Lower lobes |

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** CLE does *not* involve alveolar destruction.  
- **B:** It primarily affects upper or middle lobes, not lower.  
- **D:** CLE is typically unilateral and noncystic.  
- **E:** CLE has pulmonary, not systemic, vascular supply.  

**Correct answer: C — Congenital lobar emphysema is a developmental lesion characterized by lobar overinflation without alveolar destruction, most commonly involving the left upper lobe.**
