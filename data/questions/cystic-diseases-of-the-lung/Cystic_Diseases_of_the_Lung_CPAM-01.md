A term neonate presents with respiratory distress after birth. Chest CT demonstrates a multicystic lesion in the left lower lobe with communication to the bronchial tree and blood supply from the pulmonary artery. Histopathology reveals cysts lined by pseudostratified epithelium without normal alveoli. Which of the following best describes this lesion?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

**Congenital pulmonary airway malformation (CPAM)** represents a **developmental abnormality** of the terminal respiratory structures resulting in benign cystic lesions that replace normal alveoli. These lesions vary in size and histologic appearance but share key features that distinguish them from other congenital pulmonary anomalies.

---

### **1) Morphology and Microscopic Features**
- CPAMs consist of **macrocysts (>5 mm)** or **microcysts (<5 mm)** that represent aberrant airway structures.  
- Microscopically, the cysts are **lined by pseudostratified columnar epithelium**, resembling bronchial rather than alveolar tissue.  
- **Normal alveoli are absent**, resulting in poor gas exchange and nonfunctioning parenchyma.  
- CPAMs often appear as **hamartomatous proliferations** of terminal bronchioles.

| **Feature** | **Description** |
|--------------|----------------|
| **Histology** | Pseudostratified ciliated epithelium; no mature alveoli |
| **Cyst size** | Macrocystic (>5 mm) or microcystic (<5 mm) |
| **Function** | Poor gas exchange; nonfunctional tissue |
| **Nature** | Benign, non-neoplastic malformation |

---

### **2) Anatomic Distribution and Vascular Supply**
- **Location:** 75% occur in the **lower lobes**; bilateral or multifocal disease is rare.  
- **Vascular supply:** Derived from the **pulmonary circulation** (pulmonary arteries and veins).  
- **Airway communication:** Unlike bronchopulmonary sequestration (BPS) or bronchogenic cysts, CPAMs **communicate directly with the bronchial tree**.  
- **Hybrid lesions:** Common, combining CPAM elements with **systemic arterial supply** (BPS component). These hybrids typically **drain into pulmonary veins** but may receive systemic feeding arteries.

| **Feature** | **CPAM** | **BPS** | **Bronchogenic Cyst** |
|--------------|-----------|----------|------------------------|
| **Airway Communication** | Present | Absent | Variable |
| **Vascular Supply** | Pulmonary | Systemic | Pulmonary |
| **Drainage** | Pulmonary veins | Systemic veins | Pulmonary veins |
| **Functional Alveoli** | Absent | Absent | Absent |

---

### **3) Historical Classification — Stocker Types**
**Stocker’s classification** originally described three types (I–III), later expanded to **five types (0–4)** in a 2002 revision, reflecting the lesion’s airway origin and morphologic spectrum.

| **Type** | **Origin / Structure** | **Key Features** |
|-----------|------------------------|------------------|
| **0** | Tracheobronchial | Incompatible with life; small cysts with cartilage |
| **1** | Bronchial / bronchiolar | Large cysts (2–10 cm); most common type |
| **2** | Bronchiolar | Medium cysts (<2 cm); often associated with other anomalies |
| **3** | Bronchiolar / alveolar duct | Solid appearance (microcystic) |
| **4** | Distal acinar | Large peripheral cysts; may mimic pleuropulmonary blastoma |

- The term **“CPAM”** (Congenital Pulmonary Airway Malformation) replaced **“CCAM”** (Congenital Cystic Adenomatoid Malformation)** to emphasize the airway-based developmental origin.**  
- Despite this, both terms are still used interchangeably in clinical practice.

---

### **4) Clinical Implications**
- **Presentation:** Respiratory distress in the neonate due to mass effect, air trapping, or infection.  
- **Diagnosis:** Prenatal ultrasound and postnatal CT scan.  
- **Treatment:** Surgical lobectomy (often elective if asymptomatic).  
- **Prognosis:** Excellent after resection; malignant transformation is rare but reported in some type 4 lesions (e.g., pleuropulmonary blastoma).

---

### **5) Key Differentiating Points**
- **Communicates with bronchial tree.**  
- **Pulmonary vascular supply** (vs systemic in BPS).  
- **No functioning alveoli** → poor gas exchange.  
- **Most common congenital lung malformation.**  
- **Lower lobe predominance.**  
- **Hybrid lesions** frequently contain BPS components.
