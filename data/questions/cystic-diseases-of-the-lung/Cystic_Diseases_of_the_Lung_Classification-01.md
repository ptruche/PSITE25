A term neonate is noted to have a cystic lung lesion on prenatal ultrasound. Postnatal imaging confirms a multicystic mass in the lower lobe supplied by the systemic circulation. Histopathology later reveals both cystic airway structures and sequestrated lung parenchyma. Which of the following best describes the classification of congenital lung malformations?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

Congenital lung malformations encompass a **spectrum of developmental anomalies** involving airway, parenchymal, and vascular elements. The major recognized categories include **congenital pulmonary airway malformation (CPAM)**, **bronchopulmonary sequestration (BPS)**, **congenital lobar emphysema (CLE)**, and **bronchogenic cyst**.

---

### **1) Major Types of Congenital Lung Malformations**

| **Lesion** | **Key Features** | **Vascular Supply / Drainage** | **Common Location** |
|-------------|------------------|--------------------------------|---------------------|
| **Congenital Pulmonary Airway Malformation (CPAM)** | Cystic lesion communicating with tracheobronchial tree; abnormal airway proliferation | Pulmonary artery / pulmonary vein | Lower lobes most common |
| **Bronchopulmonary Sequestration (BPS)** | Non-functioning lung tissue without bronchial communication | Systemic arterial supply (aorta) | Intralobar (most) or extralobar |
| **Congenital Lobar Emphysema (CLE)** | Overdistended lobe due to cartilage or bronchial defect | Pulmonary circulation | Left upper or right middle lobe |
| **Bronchogenic Cyst** | Foregut duplication cyst; lined by respiratory epithelium | Pulmonary circulation | Mediastinum or lung parenchyma |

Of these, **CPAM** is the **most common** lesion identified in surgical and pathologic series.

---

### **2) Hybrid Lesions**
**Hybrid lesions** exhibit features of more than one malformation type, most frequently combining **CPAM and intralobar BPS** characteristics.  
- These lesions demonstrate **airway-type cystic elements (CPAM)** plus **systemic arterial supply (BPS)**.  
- Recognition is clinically relevant because **systemic vascular supply** can predispose to high-output cardiac failure or intraoperative hemorrhage.  
- Hybrid pathology underscores the **embryologic overlap** among congenital lung malformations.

| **Hybrid Lesion** | **Histologic Components** | **Clinical Relevance** |
|--------------------|---------------------------|------------------------|
| CPAM + Intralobar BPS | Cystic airways + systemic arterial supply | Most common hybrid form; important for surgical planning |
| CLE + CPAM | Overinflation + cystic changes | Rare; may affect management strategy |

---

### **3) Other Rare Lesions**
Less common entities include:  
- **Bronchial atresia** – focal interruption of bronchial continuity leading to mucus impaction distal to obstruction.  
- **Fetal lung interstitial tumor** – rare benign proliferation of immature mesenchyme.  
- **Pulmonary vascular malformations** – abnormal arterial or venous channels (distinct from sequestration).  

These are encountered less frequently but are part of the congenital lung malformation continuum.

---

### **4) Clinical Implications**
Understanding classification is critical because:  
- It guides **surgical decision-making** (extent of resection, need for vascular control).  
- It informs **prognosis** (risk of infection, compression, or malignancy).  
- **Hybrid lesions** must be recognized preoperatively via imaging (CT angiography or MRI) to anticipate anomalous systemic vessels.

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** Bronchogenic cysts are less common than CPAM.  
- **B:** CLE and bronchial atresia are distinct entities.  
- **D:** BPS may occur alone or as part of hybrid lesions; vascular malformations are separate conditions.  
- **E:** Hybrid lesions are common and clinically significant.  

**Correct answer: C — Congenital pulmonary airway malformation (CPAM) is the most common congenital lung malformation, and hybrid lesions frequently show features of both CPAM and intralobar bronchopulmonary sequestration.**
