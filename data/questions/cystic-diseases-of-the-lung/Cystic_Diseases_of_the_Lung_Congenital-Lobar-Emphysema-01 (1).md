A 3-month-old infant is diagnosed with congenital lobar emphysema (CLE) after prenatal imaging. The infant is asymptomatic and shows no mediastinal shift. What is the most appropriate management strategy?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

### **Management of Congenital Lobar Emphysema (CLE)**

Congenital lobar emphysema (CLE) is a rare pulmonary malformation characterized by **overinflation of one or more lobes** without true alveolar destruction. The condition often presents with **respiratory distress in early infancy**, but many cases are diagnosed incidentally or remain asymptomatic.

---

### **1) Natural History and Presentation**

- **Timing:** Most symptomatic cases present **within the first six months of life**.  
- **Symptoms:** Progressive respiratory distress, tachypnea, or cyanosis due to air trapping and mediastinal shift.  
- **Asymptomatic lesions:** May remain stable or enlarge gradually without clinical effects.

---

### **2) Diagnostic Approach**

| **Investigation** | **Purpose** |
|--------------------|-------------|
| **Chest radiograph** | Primary imaging for diagnosis and follow-up |
| **CT scan (if needed)** | Defines anatomy and rules out other cystic lesions |
| **V/Q (ventilation-perfusion) scan** | Confirms reduced ventilation and perfusion in the affected lobe |
| **PET or axial imaging** | Rarely necessary for routine monitoring |

**Key finding:** Impaired ventilation-perfusion ratio on **V/Q scan** confirms the diagnosis while excluding other abnormalities.

---

### **3) Management Strategy**

| **Clinical Status** | **Recommended Approach** | **Rationale** |
|----------------------|--------------------------|----------------|
| **Asymptomatic, no mediastinal shift** | Observation with periodic chest radiography | Safe and avoids unnecessary surgery |
| **Symptomatic (respiratory distress)** | Lobectomy | Definitive and curative treatment |
| **Asymptomatic but enlarging lesion** | Consider early lobectomy | Prevents future respiratory compromise |

- Observation is justified in **small CLEs** without **respiratory symptoms or mediastinal compression**.  
- **Lobectomy** is reserved for **symptomatic or enlarging lesions**.  
- **Serial CT scans** are not required; **chest X-rays** provide adequate follow-up.  

---

### **4) Key Points**

- CLE is often diagnosed **postnatally** when respiratory symptoms develop.  
- **Observation** is safe in asymptomatic infants.  
- **V/Q scans** confirm functional impairment and guide surgical timing.  
- **Lobectomy** is curative and typically required only for **symptomatic** cases.  
- Frequent **CT imaging** should be avoided due to radiation exposure.

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** Immediate lobectomy is not indicated for all CLE cases.  
- **B:** Serial CT scans are unnecessary for routine monitoring.  
- **D:** V/Q scans are helpful, not contraindicated.  
- **E:** Lobectomy is reserved for symptomatic or enlarging lesions, not prophylactic in all infants.  

**Correct answer: C — Observation with periodic chest radiography is appropriate for asymptomatic CLE without mediastinal shift, reserving lobectomy for symptomatic or enlarging lesions.**
