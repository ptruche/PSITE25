A 7-month-old infant with a right lower lobe congenital pulmonary airway malformation (CPAM) confined to one segment is scheduled for surgery. Which of the following statements best reflects the current standard of care regarding the extent of resection?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

### **Extent of Resection in Congenital Pulmonary Airway Malformations (CPAM)**

**Lobectomy** remains the **gold standard** for the surgical management of **CPAM**. It offers complete removal of the diseased lobe, reduces the risk of residual disease, and allows for compensatory lung growth—particularly when performed in infancy or early childhood.

---

### **1) Standard Approach: Lobectomy**

| **Feature** | **Rationale** |
|--------------|----------------|
| **Complete resection of affected lobe** | Prevents residual cysts and infection |
| **Promotes compensatory growth** | Especially effective during the alveolar phase of development |
| **Avoids recurrence** | Segmental disease may be underestimated on imaging |

Lobectomy is safe and reliable, with low long-term morbidity and excellent pulmonary function outcomes.

---

### **2) Lung-Sparing Surgery (LSR)**

**Lung-sparing surgery** (segmentectomy or wedge resection) may be considered in **select cases**, including:

| **Indication** | **Reasoning** |
|-----------------|----------------|
| **Multilobar disease** | Avoids pneumonectomy or multiple lobectomies |
| **Isolated single-segment lesion** (confirmed on high-quality CT) | Limits tissue loss when disease is well localized |
| **Older patients** | Limited potential for compensatory lung growth; minimizing resection preserves remaining function |

However, **LSR has not been proven superior** to lobectomy in terms of long-term pulmonary function or postoperative morbidity. Residual cystic disease may persist after limited resections.

---

### **3) Evidence Summary**

| **Comparison** | **Outcome** |
|----------------|-------------|
| **Lobectomy vs. LSR** | Similar morbidity and pulmonary function at long-term follow-up |
| **LSR** | Technically demanding; higher recurrence risk if lesion not completely removed |
| **Pneumonectomy** | Reserved only for diffuse, nonresectable disease |

---

### **4) Key Points**

- **Lobectomy** remains the **preferred and most studied** operation for CPAM.  
- **Lung-sparing resection** is an **option only for select patients** with limited disease and clear imaging definition.  
- **Long-term superiority of LSR has not been demonstrated.**  
- **Early resection** supports compensatory lung growth and minimizes complications.  

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** No evidence supports superior function after segmentectomy.  
- **B:** Wedge resection risks recurrence and is not standard.  
- **D:** Pneumonectomy carries high morbidity and is avoided.  
- **E:** LSR may be used in specific, limited cases.  

**Correct answer: C — Lobectomy is the standard of care for CPAM, with lung-sparing resection reserved for select cases with limited disease or high operative risk.**
