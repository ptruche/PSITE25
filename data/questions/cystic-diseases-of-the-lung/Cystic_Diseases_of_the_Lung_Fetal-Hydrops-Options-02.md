A 24-week fetus presents with hydrops and a large microcystic congenital pulmonary airway malformation (CPAM) with a CVR of 1.8. Fetal echocardiography confirms normal cardiac anatomy and function. Which of the following is the best next step in management?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

### **Management of Fetal Hydrops from Congenital Lung Lesions**

Hydrops fetalis secondary to congenital lung malformations is a **life-threatening condition** associated with **>85% mortality** if left untreated. Prompt evaluation and intervention are essential to improve fetal survival and minimize maternal risk.

---

### **1) Initial Evaluation**
- **Fetal echocardiography**: Performed to confirm **cardiac dysfunction** and exclude structural heart disease as a contributing factor.  
- **Maternal evaluation**: Assess for **mirror syndrome**, which mimics preeclampsia (hypertension, edema, proteinuria) but results from severe fetal hydrops.  
  - If mirror syndrome develops → **immediate delivery** is indicated regardless of gestational age to preserve maternal health.

| **Condition** | **Management Priority** |
|----------------|--------------------------|
| Fetal hydrops | Treat lesion and decompress mass effect |
| Maternal mirror syndrome | Immediate delivery (maternal survival priority) |

---

### **2) Maternal Corticosteroids — First-Line Therapy**
- **Indication:** **Large microcystic lesions (CVR > 1.6)** with hydrops or impending hydrops.  
- **Response rate:** **80–95%** of hydropic fetuses show significant improvement.  
- **Best outcomes:** Fetuses **<26 weeks** gestation.  
- **Mechanism:** Decreases interstitial fluid and mesenchymal proliferation, reducing lesion size and reversing venous compression.  
- **Impact:** The widespread use of maternal steroids has **drastically reduced the need** for open fetal surgery.

| **Lesion Type** | **Effect of Steroids** |
|------------------|-----------------------|
| Microcystic CPAM | Marked improvement (80–95%) |
| Macrocystic CPAM | Minimal response |
| BPS | Variable response |

---

### **3) Thoracoamniotic Shunting — For Macrocystic Lesions**
- **Indication:** **Dominant macrocystic CPAM** that is **unresponsive to steroids**.  
- **Goal:** Drains cystic fluid to relieve mediastinal compression and venous obstruction.  
- **Technique:** Ultrasound-guided placement of a **2-mm Silastic double-pigtail catheter** between the cyst and the amniotic cavity.  
- **Complications:**  
  - Premature rupture of membranes (PPROM).  
  - Preterm labor or chorioamnionitis.  
  - Shunt obstruction or dislodgment.  

---

### **4) Fetal Lung Resection — Last-Resort Therapy**
- **Indication:** **Pre-viable or borderline viable** fetuses with **solid lesions (microcystic CPAM or BPS)** **unresponsive to steroids**.  
- Requires **maternal laparotomy, hysterotomy, and fetal thoracotomy**.  
- **Risks:** Major maternal hemorrhage, preterm delivery, and **~50% fetal mortality**.  
- **Alternatives:** Laser ablation or bipolar coagulation have been used experimentally but may initially worsen edema before improvement.

---

### **5) Summary Table: Management of Hydropic Fetuses**

| **Therapy** | **Indication** | **Response Rate** | **Risks / Limitations** |
|--------------|----------------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| **Maternal steroids** | Microcystic CPAM (CVR > 1.6) | 80–95% improvement | Limited effect in macrocystic lesions |
| **Thoracoamniotic shunt** | Macrocystic CPAM, steroid failure | Variable | PPROM, infection, shunt dislodgment |
| **Open fetal resection** | Pre-viable, steroid nonresponders | ~50% survival | High maternal and fetal morbidity |

---

### **6) Clinical Pearls**
- **Hydropic fetuses require urgent intervention.**  
- **Maternal corticosteroids** are the **first-line therapy** for **microcystic lesions**, while **shunting** is reserved for **macrocystic or refractory cases**.  
- **Open fetal resection** is a last-resort option for **solid lesions** unresponsive to other therapy.  
- If **maternal mirror syndrome** develops, **immediate delivery** is mandatory.
