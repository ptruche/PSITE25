A 25-week pregnant patient presents with a fetus diagnosed with a large microcystic congenital pulmonary airway malformation (CPAM) and nonimmune hydrops. Fetal echocardiography confirms normal cardiac anatomy and function. Which of the following is the most appropriate initial management?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

### **Management of Hydropic Fetuses with Congenital Lung Lesions**

Hydrops fetalis in the setting of a **fetal lung malformation** signifies **severe cardiovascular compromise** due to mass effect and venous obstruction. Without intervention, **mortality exceeds 85%**.

---

### **1) Initial Evaluation**
- **Fetal echocardiography** is essential to confirm **cardiac dysfunction** and **exclude congenital heart disease**.  
- **Maternal evaluation** is also critical to detect **mirror syndrome**, a life-threatening condition with **maternal hypertension, edema, and proteinuria**.  
  - If mirror syndrome develops, **immediate delivery** is indicated **regardless of gestational age** to preserve maternal life.

---

### **2) First-Line Therapy: Maternal Corticosteroids**
- **Maternal steroids (e.g., betamethasone)** are **first-line treatment** for **microcystic lesions** with **CVR > 1.6** and **hydrops or impending hydrops**.  
- Steroids have shown **benefit in 80–95%** of such fetuses, often reducing lesion size and improving venous return.  
- **Mechanism:** Induce regression of mesenchymal proliferation and improve fetal lung fluid dynamics.  
- **Best outcomes:** Fetuses **<26 weeks gestation**.  
- Use of steroids has **dramatically reduced the need** for open fetal resection.  
- **Limitations:** Minimal effect in **macrocystic lesions**, which likely have distinct developmental biology.

| **Lesion Type** | **Response to Steroids** |
|------------------|---------------------------|
| Microcystic CPAM | 80–95% beneficial effect |
| Macrocystic CPAM | Poor response |
| BPS | Variable response |

---

### **3) Second-Line Therapy: Thoracoamniotic Shunting**
- Indicated for **macrocystic CPAMs** or **dominant macrocystic lesions** that do **not respond to steroids**.  
- The shunt drains cyst fluid into the amniotic cavity, reducing mass effect and venous obstruction.  
- **Technique:** Ultrasound-guided placement of a **2-mm double pigtail Silastic catheter**.  
- **Risks:**  
  - **Preterm premature rupture of membranes (PPROM)**.  
  - **Preterm labor**.  
  - **Chorioamnionitis**.  
  - **Shunt occlusion or dislodgment**.  

| **Indication** | **Procedure** | **Complications** |
|-----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Macrocystic CPAM unresponsive to steroids | Thoracoamniotic shunt | PPROM, infection, dislodgment |

---

### **4) Third-Line Therapy: Open Fetal Resection**
- Reserved for **pre-viable or borderline viable hydropic fetuses** with **solid (microcystic) lesions** unresponsive to steroids.  
- Requires **maternal laparotomy, hysterotomy, and fetal thoracotomy** under **uterine relaxation**.  
- **Risks:** Significant **maternal hemorrhage**, **preterm labor**, and **~50% mortality**.  
- Typically performed only in **specialized fetal centers**.  
- **Less invasive alternatives** (e.g., laser coagulation, radiofrequency ablation) have been trialed but may **worsen edema initially** before improvement.

---

### **5) Summary Table: Fetal Interventions for Hydrops**

| **Therapy** | **Indication** | **Success Rate / Outcome** |
|--------------|----------------|-----------------------------|
| **Maternal steroids** | First-line for microcystic CPAM with CVR > 1.6 or hydrops | 80–95% improvement |
| **Thoracoamniotic shunt** | Macrocystic CPAM, poor steroid response | Variable; high PPROM risk |
| **Open fetal resection** | Solid lesions, pre-viable, steroid nonresponders | ~50% survival; high morbidity |

---

### **6) Key Management Priorities**
- Confirm **cardiac function** (via echocardiography).  
- Treat **maternal mirror syndrome** emergently if present.  
- **Steroids first** → **Shunt if macrocystic or steroid failure** → **Fetal resection only if refractory**.  
- Coordinate care at a **multidisciplinary fetal therapy center**.

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** Open resection is a last resort, not first-line.  
- **B:** Steroids are most effective *before* 26 weeks, not contraindicated.  
- **C:** Shunting is indicated for macrocystic lesions, not microcystic.  
- **E:** Expectant management has >85% mortality.  

**Correct answer: D — Maternal corticosteroids are first-line for large microcystic lesions with hydrops; thoracoamniotic shunt or fetal resection are reserved for nonresponders or macrocystic lesions.**
