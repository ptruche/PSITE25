A 5-year-old child with a small congenital pulmonary airway malformation (CPAM) and a recent episode of pneumonia is being evaluated for elective resection. He currently has persistent productive cough and fever despite one week of oral antibiotics. What is the most appropriate next step in management?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

### **Contraindications to Immediate Lung Resection**

Lung resection, while curative for congenital lung malformations such as **CPAM, bronchopulmonary sequestration (BPS),** and **bronchogenic cyst**, should **not** be performed in the setting of **active infection** or when the procedure would lead to unacceptable loss of pulmonary function.

---

### **1) Active Infection**

- **Pneumonia** or **pulmonary abscess** should be treated **before surgery**.  
- **Antibiotic therapy** is the mainstay of treatment.  
- **Percutaneous drainage** is reserved for cases refractory to antibiotics.  
- Surgery during infection increases the risk of **bronchopleural fistula** and **prolonged air leaks**.

| **Infectious State** | **Management Before Surgery** |
|----------------------|--------------------------------|
| Pneumonia | Treat with antibiotics until resolution |
| Pulmonary abscess | Drain if nonresponsive to antibiotics, then delay resection |
| Empyema | Drain and treat before elective resection |

---

### **2) Situations Warranting Deferred Resection**

| **Scenario** | **Reason to Defer Surgery** |
|---------------|-----------------------------|
| **Multifocal CPAM in asymptomatic patient** | Multiple lobectomies would severely reduce total lung volume |
| **Older patient with poor pulmonary reserve** | Limited benefit, increased operative risk |
| **Patient with severe comorbidities** (e.g. single ventricle physiology) | Cardiac risk outweighs benefit of resection |
| **Ongoing or unresolved infection** | Increases operative morbidity and risk of postoperative complications |

---

### **3) Surgical Timing**

- Resection should be planned **after infection resolution** and **optimization of respiratory function**.  
- **Elective resection** of asymptomatic lesions can occur in infancy or early childhood to allow for compensatory lung growth.  
- In patients with **high operative risk or marginal benefit**, an **observation strategy** with imaging follow-up may be appropriate.  

---

### **4) Key Points**

- **Avoid surgery during active infection.**  
- **Treat infection first** with antibiotics ± drainage.  
- **Defer resection** if pulmonary function is already compromised or surgical risk outweighs benefit.  
- **Surgery during active inflammation** increases morbidity and complicates resection due to distorted anatomy.
