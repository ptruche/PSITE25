A full-term infant was diagnosed prenatally with a right lower lobe cystic lung lesion. The newborn is currently asymptomatic and feeding comfortably. Chest radiograph shows no mass effect or pneumothorax. Which of the following best describes the appropriate evaluation strategy for this infant?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

**Evaluation of newborns with prenatally diagnosed congenital lung malformations (CLMs)** depends on **symptom severity** at birth. The diagnostic approach balances the need for accurate anatomic characterization against the risks of early imaging and radiation exposure.

---

### **1) Symptomatic Infants**
- **Initial study:** **Plain chest radiograph** — quickly identifies lesion laterality and rules out emergent issues (e.g., tension pneumothorax, mediastinal shift).  
- **If unstable:** Radiograph alone may suffice before proceeding to **life-saving thoracotomy**.  
- **If stable:** Perform **multidetector CT with angiography (MDCTA)** to:  
  - Define **anatomic extent** and **cystic morphology**.  
  - Identify **systemic vascular supply** (e.g., sequestration).  
  - Guide **surgical planning**.

**Adjunctive imaging:**  
- **Echocardiography** may assess:  
  - **Pulmonary hypertension** (for anesthetic and perioperative management).  
  - **Associated congenital cardiac anomalies** (if suggested prenatally).

| **Symptom status** | **Initial Imaging** | **Definitive Imaging** | **Adjunct** |
|---------------------|---------------------|-------------------------|--------------|
| Symptomatic & unstable | Plain film | — | — |
| Symptomatic & stable | Plain film | MDCTA | Echocardiography |

---

### **2) Asymptomatic Infants**
- **Initial step:** **Single-view chest radiograph** after birth to exclude urgent conditions such as:  
  - **Large space-occupying lesion**.  
  - **Mediastinal shift**.  
  - **Pneumothorax.**  
- If these are absent, the infant may be **discharged** and scheduled for **delayed imaging**.  
- **Definitive evaluation:** **MDCTA** at **4–6 weeks postnatal age** to allow resolution of:  
  - **Fetal lung fluid**.  
  - **Atelectasis**, which can obscure cystic detail.  

**Rationale:** Early neonatal CT may misinterpret **fluid-filled cysts** as normal lung tissue; delayed imaging improves diagnostic accuracy and surgical planning.

| **Timing** | **Reason for Delay** | **Optimal Modality** |
|-------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| 4–6 weeks postnatal | Clearance of fetal lung fluid; improved aeration | MDCTA |

---

### **3) Role of MDCTA**
- **Advantages:**  
  - **High-resolution imaging** with 3D reconstruction.  
  - **Vascular mapping** to identify systemic supply.  
  - Eliminates the need for separate angiography or ultrasound.  
- **Best practice:** Use **ALARA (As Low As Reasonably Achievable)** protocols to minimize radiation exposure.

---

### **4) Imaging Pitfalls**
- **Normal radiograph ≠ normal lung:** does **not exclude** a lesion seen prenatally.  
- **Immediate neonatal CT:** limited by **fetal lung fluid and atelectasis**.  
- **Ultrasound:** not reliable for postnatal diagnosis due to poor sonographic windows.

---

### **5) Summary Table**

| **Clinical Status** | **Initial Imaging** | **Definitive Imaging** | **Timing** | **Notes** |
|----------------------|---------------------|-------------------------|-------------|------------|
| **Symptomatic (stable)** | CXR | MDCTA | Immediate | Define extent & vasculature |
| **Symptomatic (unstable)** | CXR | — | Immediate | Thoracotomy if life-threatening |
| **Asymptomatic** | CXR | MDCTA | 4–6 weeks | Delayed for improved visualization |

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** Immediate CT is unnecessary and may yield suboptimal results.  
- **B:** Echocardiography is safe and indicated selectively.  
- **D:** Normal CXR does *not* exclude a lesion.  
- **E:** Postnatal imaging confirmation is always required.  

**Correct answer: C — Symptomatic newborns require urgent plain radiography followed by MDCTA when stable, while asymptomatic infants should undergo delayed CT angiography (4–6 weeks) for optimal evaluation.**
