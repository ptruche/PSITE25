A 5-year-old child presents with recurrent pneumonia localized to the same lung segment. CT scan reveals a multicystic lesion in the left lower lobe with normal pulmonary arterial supply. The child had no prior prenatal diagnosis. Which of the following statements best characterizes the presentation and complications of congenital lung malformations in older children?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

**Congenital lung malformations (CLMs)** such as **congenital pulmonary airway malformation (CPAM)**, **bronchopulmonary sequestration (BPS)**, and **bronchogenic cysts** may remain asymptomatic for years and are increasingly identified incidentally on imaging performed for other reasons (e.g., trauma, abdominal pain). However, when symptomatic, **recurrent pulmonary infections** are the most common clinical presentation.

---

### **1) Common Presentations in Older Children**
- **Incidental findings:** Many lesions are discovered **incidentally** on CT scans performed for unrelated issues.  
- **Recurrent infections:** The **most frequent symptomatic presentation** in older children.  
  - **CPAM:** recurrent pneumonia due to **poor mucociliary clearance** and **bacterial colonization** of abnormal cystic tissue.  
  - **Intralobar BPS:** infection due to **collateral ventilation** from adjacent normal lung through the **pores of Kohn**.  
- **Timing:** Pneumonia related to CPAM typically appears **after 9 months of age**.  
- **Course:** Historically described as **complex, recurrent, and difficult to treat** with antibiotics.

| **Lesion Type** | **Mechanism of Infection** | **Presentation** |
|------------------|----------------------------|------------------|
| **CPAM** | Poor clearance in cystic tissue | Recurrent pneumonia |
| **Intralobar BPS** | Collateral ventilation from adjacent lung | Recurrent pneumonia |
| **Extralobar BPS** | Isolated from airway, rarely infected | Incidental mass |

---

### **2) Other Complications**
Although rare, CLMs can produce serious **cardiopulmonary and neoplastic complications:**  
- **Large intralobar/extralobar BPS:** chronic systemic shunting → **high-output cardiac failure**.  
- **Bronchogenic cysts:** progressive **mucus accumulation**, **infection**, and **airway compression**.  
- **Malignant transformation:**  
  - **CPAM → adenocarcinoma** (rare, mostly in older children or adults).  
  - **Pleuropulmonary blastoma (PPB):** pediatric malignancy that may mimic CPAM, especially **macrocystic lesions diagnosed postnatally**.

| **Complication** | **Typical Lesion** | **Notes** |
|-------------------|--------------------|------------|
| Recurrent pneumonia | CPAM, intralobar BPS | Common indication for resection |
| High-output heart failure | Large BPS | Systemic shunting |
| Airway obstruction | Bronchogenic cyst | Due to enlargement or infection |
| Malignant degeneration | CPAM (rare) | Adenocarcinoma / PPB |

---

### **3) Differentiating CPAM from Pleuropulmonary Blastoma (PPB)**
Distinguishing CPAM from early PPB is crucial since **PPB carries malignant potential** and may mimic cystic lung lesions radiographically.

| **Feature** | **CPAM** | **Pleuropulmonary Blastoma (PPB)** |
|--------------|-----------|------------------------------------|
| **Timing of diagnosis** | Prenatal / neonatal | Postnatal |
| **Laterality** | Usually unilateral, single lesion | Often bilateral or multifocal |
| **Family history** | None | May be positive for PPB or DICER1 mutation |
| **Pneumothorax association** | Rare | Common |
| **Histology** | Hamartomatous epithelium | Malignant mesenchymal elements |
| **Median age (Type I PPB)** | — | ~8 months |

**Factors suggesting PPB over CPAM:**  
- Postnatal diagnosis.  
- Bilateral or multisegment involvement.  
- Complicating pneumothorax.  
- Family history of PPB.  

---

### **4) Clinical Management**
- **Asymptomatic lesions:** often resected electively to prevent infection or malignancy.  
- **Symptomatic lesions:** surgical resection indicated after infection resolution.  
- **Bronchogenic cysts:** resected to prevent infection or compression.  
- **Monitoring:** postoperative follow-up to ensure compensatory lung growth.

---

### **5) Key Takeaways**
- Recurrent pneumonia is the **hallmark presentation** in older children.  
- CPAM and intralobar BPS are the **most infection-prone**.  
- Large BPS → **systemic shunting** → **heart failure**.  
- Bronchogenic cysts → **infection or compression** over time.  
- **PPB must be ruled out** in any **postnatally diagnosed cystic lesion**.
