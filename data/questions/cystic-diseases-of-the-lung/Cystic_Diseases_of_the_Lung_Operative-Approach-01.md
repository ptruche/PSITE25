A 4-month-old infant with a congenital pulmonary airway malformation (CPAM) is scheduled for elective resection. The patient is stable, and the surgeon is experienced in minimally invasive techniques. Which of the following statements best reflects current evidence on operative approach?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

### **Operative Approach for Pediatric Lung Resection**

The **optimal operative approach** for congenital lung lesions such as **CPAM**, **bronchopulmonary sequestration**, or **congenital lobar emphysema (CLE)** remains **individualized**, as no randomized data support the superiority of thoracoscopic versus open resection. Both techniques have excellent long-term outcomes when applied appropriately.

---

### **1) Choice of Approach**

| **Approach** | **Indications / Advantages** | **Limitations** |
|---------------|------------------------------|-----------------|
| **Thoracoscopic resection** | Excellent visualization; smaller incisions; potentially reduced pain and recovery time | Requires tolerance of single-lung ventilation and stable physiology; limited working space in small infants |
| **Open muscle-sparing thoracotomy** | Reliable, safe exposure; preferred in unstable patients or with CLE | Slightly longer recovery; historical concern for chest wall deformity (mitigated with muscle-sparing techniques) |

---

### **2) Key Evidence and Modern Practice**

- Nearly **half of all pediatric lung resections** are now performed **thoracoscopically**.  
- **No modern studies** have shown clear superiority of thoracoscopic or open approaches regarding postoperative outcomes, complications, or pulmonary function.  
- **Surgeon experience and patient stability** are the primary determinants of approach.  
- In **CLE**, thoracoscopic surgery is **not preferred** due to difficulty maintaining working space and anesthetic challenges.  

---

### **3) Open vs Thoracoscopic Outcomes**

| **Feature** | **Thoracoscopic** | **Modern Open (Muscle-Sparing)** |
|--------------|------------------|----------------------------------|
| Postoperative pain | Slightly lower (subjective) | Minimal with modern techniques |
| Cosmetic result | Better | Acceptable |
| Risk of chest wall deformity | Minimal | Rare with modern muscle-sparing approach |
| Safety in instability | Limited | Excellent |

**Modern open thoracotomies** avoid **latissimus division** and **rib resection**, minimizing risk for deformity and chronic pain.  

---

### **4) Summary Points**

- **Both thoracoscopic and open approaches are acceptable**; decision is **patient and surgeon-dependent**.  
- **Thoracoscopic**: For stable patients, experienced surgeons.  
- **Open (muscle-sparing)**: For unstable patients or those intolerant of single-lung ventilation (e.g. CLE).  
- **Chest wall deformities** associated with old thoracotomy techniques are **rare with modern methods**.  

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** Thoracoscopic surgery is not universally superior.  
- **B:** Rib resection and muscle division are obsolete.  
- **D:** Thoracoscopy can be performed safely in infants by experienced surgeons.  
- **E:** Latissimus division is unnecessary with modern muscle-sparing approaches.  

**Correct answer: C — The choice between thoracoscopic and open resection depends on patient stability, ability to tolerate single-lung ventilation, and surgeon experience.**
