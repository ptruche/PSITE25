A 2-month-old infant was prenatally diagnosed with a small cystic lung lesion and remains asymptomatic. What is the most appropriate next step in management according to current patient care guidelines?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

### **Patient Care Guidelines for Congenital Pulmonary Malformations**

#### **Acutely Symptomatic Infant**
- **Initial assessment:** Chest radiograph.  
- **Next step:** **Immediate resection via thoracotomy** is indicated if the infant shows respiratory distress due to mass effect, infection, or pneumothorax.  
- **Goal:** Rapid decompression and stabilization.

---

#### **Asymptomatic Infant**
1. **Initial evaluation:** Physical exam and chest radiograph.  
2. **Imaging:** **High-definition chest CT** at **3–6 months of age** for anatomic delineation.  
3. **Family counseling:**  
   - Discuss options for **elective surgery versus observation**.  
   - Decision influenced by lesion size, growth, and family preference.  
4. **Surgical considerations:**  
   - **Lobectomy** is standard; **segmentectomy** may be considered in select cases.  
   - Approach can be **open or thoracoscopic**, depending on surgeon experience and patient stability.  
5. **Postoperative course:**  
   - **2–5 day hospital stay.**  
   - **Routine follow-up** at 3–4 weeks postoperatively with a chest radiograph.  
   - **Long-term follow-up** by surgeon or pediatrician individualized based on findings.

---

#### **Late Diagnosis**
- **Initial workup:** Chest radiograph.  
- **CT imaging** after infection resolution if pneumonia was the presenting symptom.  
- **Definitive management:** Resection after inflammation subsides.  
- **Postoperative care:** 2–5 day hospitalization and 3–4 week follow-up with imaging.

---

#### **Key Takeaways**

| **Clinical Setting** | **Initial Step** | **Definitive Management** | **Follow-Up** |
|----------------------|------------------|----------------------------|----------------|
| **Acutely symptomatic infant** | Chest radiograph | Immediate thoracotomy | 3–4 week follow-up with CXR |
| **Asymptomatic infant** | CXR, CT at 3–6 mo | Elective open or thoracoscopic resection vs observation | Routine imaging follow-up |
| **Late diagnosis** | CXR ± CT | Resection after infection resolution | 2–5 day stay, follow-up imaging |

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** Immediate resection is not indicated in all cases.  
- **C:** Observation is acceptable for small, asymptomatic lesions.  
- **D:** Emergent surgery is only for symptomatic patients.  
- **E:** Long-term follow-up is individualized but not unnecessary.  

**Correct answer: B — Asymptomatic infants should undergo chest CT at 3–6 months followed by individualized discussion of surgery versus observation.**
