A 6-month-old infant undergoes thoracoscopic resection of a left lower lobe intralobar sequestration. Postoperatively, a small pneumothorax is noted after chest tube removal, and the child remains clinically stable. Which of the following statements best reflects the management principles and expected complications after lung resection in congenital pulmonary malformations?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

### **Postoperative Complications after Lung Resection for Congenital Lesions**

Although **pediatric lung resection** is generally safe, complications occur in **5–15%** of cases and are paradoxically **more common after segmentectomy** than after **lobectomy**.

---

### **1) Major Postoperative Complications and Incidence**

| **Complication** | **Incidence (%)** | **Notes** |
|------------------|------------------|------------|
| **Bleeding requiring transfusion** | 3.7–11 | Often related to aberrant systemic vessels (esp. in sequestration) |
| **Prolonged mechanical ventilation (>48 hr)** | ~8 | Seen in complex or large resections |
| **Surgical site infection** | ~1.2 | Rare due to perioperative antibiotics |
| **Prolonged air leak** | 1.2–4 | More frequent after segmentectomy |
| **Nerve injury (temporary/permanent)** | ~4 | Intercostal or phrenic nerve involvement |
| **Bronchial plug / atelectasis** | — | From mucus retention or poor pulmonary toilet |

---

### **2) Intraoperative Bleeding**

- A common source is the **systemic feeding vessel** in sequestration.  
- **Preventive strategies:**  
  - **Identify and ligate** the vessel early before traction or dissection.  
  - In open cases, use **double ligation** and **suture control**.  
  - **Energy-sealing devices** (e.g. LigaSure®) effectively reduce bleeding during parenchymal division.

---

### **3) Pneumothorax Management**

| **Situation** | **Management** |
|----------------|----------------|
| **Small, stable pneumothorax** | Observation and repeat imaging; often resolves spontaneously |
| **Large pneumothorax or air leak** | Replace chest tube and maintain suction for 48 hrs |

Ensure **airtight dressing** at tube removal to prevent recurrence.

---

### **4) Recurrent or Persistent Cystic Lesions**

- Seen in **1.2–15%** of cases.  
- More common after **segmental resection** due to incomplete removal or compensatory emphysema.  
- Evaluate with **CT scan** to differentiate:  
  - **True recurrence** → consider repeat thoracoscopy.  
  - **Compensatory emphysema** → benign, observe.  

---

### **5) Key Points**

- Complications are **more frequent in segmentectomy** due to incomplete planes and smaller working margins.  
- **Bleeding control** during sequestration surgery is crucial; early ligation of feeding vessels prevents major hemorrhage.  
- **Small postoperative pneumothoraces** can be observed safely.  
- **Persistent cystic lesions** warrant imaging before deciding on reoperation.  

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** Segmentectomy actually carries higher complication rates than lobectomy.  
- **B:** Not all pneumothoraces require chest tube reinsertion.  
- **C:** Aberrant systemic vessels are a common bleeding source.  
- **E:** Persistent cysts must be evaluated; many are benign emphysematous changes.  

**Correct answer: D — Complications such as bleeding, prolonged air leak, and pneumothorax are more common after segmental resection than lobectomy, often related to disruption of aberrant vessels or incomplete sealing of parenchyma.**
