A 2-month-old infant with a large right lower lobe congenital pulmonary airway malformation (CPAM) is scheduled for an elective lobectomy. Which of the following summarizes the key elements of appropriate preoperative preparation?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

### **Comprehensive Preoperative Preparation for Congenital Lung Resection**

Proper preoperative preparation ensures **surgical safety**, **airway control**, and **optimized postoperative recovery**. Planning must be multidisciplinary, involving **pediatric surgery, anesthesia, and critical care**.

---

### **1) Imaging**

- **Multidetector CT with Angiography (MDCTA)** is essential for:  
  - Identifying the **affected lobe**  
  - Detecting **systemic vascular supply** (e.g., sequestration vessels)  
- Imaging defines **operative anatomy** and assists in determining surgical feasibility and approach.

---

### **2) Anticipate Postoperative Care**

- Patients with large lesions, pulmonary hypertension, or chronic lung disease may require **ICU-level postoperative monitoring** and **mechanical ventilation**.  
- Early planning for **intensive care support** improves outcomes.

---

### **3) Pulmonary Optimization**

- Maximize **pulmonary toilet** and **treat reactive airway disease** before surgery.  
- Optimize oxygenation and secretion clearance to reduce perioperative complications.

---

### **4) Laboratory and Transfusion Preparation**

| **Assessment** | **Purpose** |
|----------------|-------------|
| **Type and Screen** | Anticipate possible intraoperative transfusion |
| **Routine blood work** | Not required unless clinically indicated |

---

### **5) Anesthetic Planning**

- **Close coordination** with anesthesia is vital, especially for large CPAMs or **congenital lobar emphysema (CLE)**.  
- Anticipate **difficult ventilation** and risk of **tension physiology** during induction.  
- **Gentle hand ventilation** before thoracotomy prevents overdistention.  
- **Selective endobronchial intubation** of the contralateral lung may temporize until resection is complete.  
- Secure **durable IV** and **arterial access** for fluid resuscitation and blood gas monitoring.

---

### **6) Equipment Preparation**

- For **minimally invasive surgery**:  
  - Use **3–5 mm sealing devices** for vascular control.  
  - **5 mm staplers** facilitate bronchial division.  
- Ensure availability of open instruments for conversion if needed.

---

### **7) Antibiotic Prophylaxis**

| **Agent** | **Timing** | **Duration** |
|------------|-------------|--------------|
| **Cefazolin** | Within 30 minutes before incision | Two additional doses, 8 hours apart |
| **Alternatives (allergy)** | Clindamycin or Vancomycin | Same timing |

- Lobectomy is a **clean-contaminated procedure**.  
- **Prolonged antibiotics while the chest tube is in place are not indicated.**

---

### **8) Key Points**

- **CT angiography** defines anatomy and vascular supply.  
- **Pulmonary optimization** reduces complications.  
- **Coordination with anesthesia** ensures safe single-lung ventilation.  
- **Perioperative antibiotics** should be administered within 30 minutes of incision.  
- **Routine labs** are unnecessary except for **type and screen**.  

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** Routine labs are not universally required.  
- **B:** Single-lung ventilation is often necessary with proper anesthetic control.  
- **D:** Cephalosporins remain first-line prophylaxis.  
- **E:** Extended antibiotics are not supported by evidence.  

**Correct answer: C — Preoperative preparation includes accurate imaging with CT angiography, pulmonary optimization, airway planning, and prophylactic antibiotics within 30 minutes of incision.**
