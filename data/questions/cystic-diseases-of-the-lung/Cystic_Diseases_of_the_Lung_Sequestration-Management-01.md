A 3-month-old infant with a prenatally diagnosed left lower lobe intralobar sequestration (ILS) is planned for elective surgical management. Which of the following statements best reflects the optimal treatment approach?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

### **Management of Intralobar and Extralobar Bronchopulmonary Sequestration (BPS)**

**Bronchopulmonary sequestration (BPS)** is a congenital malformation characterized by nonfunctional lung tissue that receives **systemic arterial blood supply** and lacks connection with the tracheobronchial tree.

---

### **1) Treatment Principles**

| **Type** | **Pleural Relationship** | **Venous Drainage** | **Preferred Treatment** |
|-----------|--------------------------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| **Intralobar Sequestration (ILS)** | Within visceral pleura of normal lung | Pulmonary veins | **Lobectomy** of affected lobe |
| **Extralobar Sequestration (ELS)** | Separate pleural covering | Systemic veins | **Excision after control of systemic artery** |

- **ILS:** Lobectomy is necessary because the plane between normal and abnormal lung tissue is poorly defined.  
- **ELS:** Excision is performed after careful **isolation and ligation of systemic arterial feeders**, typically arising from the **thoracic or abdominal aorta**.  

---

### **2) Operative Approach**

| **Approach** | **Indications** |
|---------------|----------------|
| **Thoracoscopic resection** | Stable patients, experienced surgeon, clear anatomy |
| **Open thoracotomy** | Poor tolerance of single-lung ventilation or pneumothorax required for thoracoscopy |

Modern **muscle-sparing thoracotomy** minimizes morbidity and avoids historical chest wall deformities.

---

### **3) Role of Coil Embolization**

- **Not first-line therapy** — does **not remove dysplastic tissue** and carries risk of **embolization complications** (e.g., infarction, infection).  
- **Useful only in select cases:**  
  - Palliative treatment for **hemoptysis** from unresected ILS.  
  - **Preoperative adjunct** for large feeding vessels that are technically challenging to control.  

---

### **4) Key Points**

- **Lobectomy** is the standard for **ILS** due to lack of tissue plane.  
- **ELS** requires **arterial control** prior to excision.  
- **Thoracoscopic resection** is appropriate for stable patients; open approach for unstable or technically difficult cases.  
- **Embolization** is reserved for limited palliative or adjunctive scenarios.  

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** Coil embolization is not first-line; it does not remove the lesion.  
- **B:** ELS should still be resected to prevent future complications.  
- **C:** Lobectomy is required for ILS due to poor separation of diseased and normal lung.  
- **E:** Thoracoscopic resection is safe in experienced hands.  

**Correct answer: D — Lobectomy is the treatment for intralobar sequestration, while extralobar sequestration requires control of the systemic arterial supply before excision.**
