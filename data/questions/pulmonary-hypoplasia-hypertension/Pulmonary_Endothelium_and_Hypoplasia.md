A neonate with **congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH)** is found to have **severe pulmonary hypertension**. Laboratory and histologic studies show **increased endothelin-1 (ET-1)** levels, **decreased nitric oxide (NO)** availability, and **reactive oxygen species (ROS)** that inactivate NO. Lung biopsy reveals **reduced airway branching**, **thickened vascular walls**, and **fewer alveoli**.

Which of the following mechanisms best explains the **pathogenesis of pulmonary hypertension** in this infant?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

The **pulmonary vascular endothelium** regulates **vascular tone** and **resistance** via the balance between **vasodilators** (NO, prostacyclin) and **vasoconstrictors** (ET-1). In pulmonary hypertension, this balance is disrupted:  
- **NO and prostacyclin** synthesis is **decreased**, reducing vasodilation.  
- **Endothelin-1** and **ROS** are **increased**, promoting **vasoconstriction** and **smooth muscle proliferation**.

In **pulmonary hypoplasia** (e.g., CDH, oligohydramnios), the **vascular bed is underdeveloped**, with **fewer, thicker arterioles** and **reduced capillary cross-sectional area**, further increasing **PVR**.

### Key Takeaways
- **Endothelial dysfunction** is a major driver of neonatal pulmonary hypertension.  
- **↑ET-1, ↓NO, ↑ROS** → vasoconstriction and vascular remodeling.  
- **Pulmonary hypoplasia** decreases vascular surface area → worsens PVR.  
- These mechanisms underlie **refractory pulmonary hypertension** seen in CDH and similar disorders.  

### Why the Other Options Are Wrong
- **A.** NO synthase activity is *reduced*, not increased.  
- **C.** Hypoplastic lungs have *fewer* alveoli and *thicker* capillary walls.  
- **D.** ROS *inactivate* NO, worsening vasoconstriction.  
- **E.** Surfactant deficiency and low elastin *increase* PVR.  

**Correct answer: B — Endothelin-1 upregulation and nitric oxide deficiency promote pulmonary vasoconstriction and increased PVR.**
