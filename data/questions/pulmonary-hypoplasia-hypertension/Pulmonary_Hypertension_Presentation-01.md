A term infant develops persistent hypoxemia and mild respiratory distress shortly after birth. The chest radiograph shows clear lung fields with mild hyperinflation and decreased vascular markings. Echocardiography reveals a structurally normal heart with right-to-left shunting across the ductus arteriosus and right ventricular strain. Which of the following findings best characterizes this patient’s condition?  

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

**Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN)** is a physiologic syndrome of **elevated pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR)** that causes **right-to-left shunting** through fetal channels (foramen ovale and ductus arteriosus) and results in **hypoxemia despite adequate lung aeration**. It represents a failure of normal postnatal transition from fetal to neonatal circulation.

---

### **1) Pathophysiologic Basis**
After birth, pulmonary vascular resistance normally falls dramatically as oxygen tension rises and lung aeration occurs. In PPHN, this fall is **incomplete or reversed**, leaving a **high-resistance pulmonary circuit**. Consequently:
- Blood flow through the lungs is reduced.  
- The right ventricle ejects into systemic circulation through fetal shunts.  
- Oxygen-poor blood mixes into the systemic circulation, causing **cyanosis and hypoxemia**.

---

### **2) Clinical Presentation in the Neonate**
The hallmark clinical features reflect **right heart strain and impaired oxygen exchange**:

| **Feature** | **Mechanism** | **Clinical Manifestation** |
|--------------|----------------|-----------------------------|
| **Hypoxemia** | Right-to-left shunting at PDA or PFO | Cyanosis, low SpO₂ unresponsive to ventilation |
| **Pre/Post-Ductal Saturation Gradient** | Differential shunting between upper (preductal) and lower (postductal) body | >5% difference in O₂ saturation |
| **Hypercarbia** | Inadequate perfusion and ventilation mismatch | Elevated PaCO₂ |
| **Right Ventricular Strain** | Pressure overload of thin-walled RV | Tachycardia, gallop, hepatomegaly |
| **Decreased LV Preload** | Reduced pulmonary venous return | Systemic hypotension |

---

### **3) Diagnostic Findings**
- **Chest Radiograph:** Mild hyperinflation and **paucity of vascular markings** (reflecting low pulmonary blood flow). Minimal parenchymal disease differentiates PPHN from pneumonia or meconium aspiration.  
- **Echocardiography:**  
  - Normal cardiac anatomy (rules out CHD).  
  - **Right-to-left or bidirectional shunting** across PFO/PDA.  
  - **Right ventricular hypertrophy or dilation**.  
  - **Tricuspid regurgitant jet** (used to estimate RV systolic pressure).  
  - Flattened or **left-bowed interventricular septum**, indicating high RV pressure.  
- **Pulse Oximetry:** Pre- and post-ductal saturation difference >5–10% that **improves with oxygen** suggests a **reactive pulmonary vascular bed**.

---

### **4) Pathophysiologic Consequences**
- **Right-to-left shunting** bypasses the pulmonary circuit, causing **hypoxemia**.  
- **Reduced LV preload** (from low pulmonary venous return) leads to **systemic hypotension**.  
- **Hypercarbia and acidosis** further worsen pulmonary vasoconstriction, perpetuating a **vicious cycle**.  

---

### **5) Presentation in Older Children or Adolescents**
In older patients, pulmonary hypertension is usually **secondary to another condition**:
- **Pneumonia or lung disease** → hypoxia and reactive pulmonary vasoconstriction.  
- **Chronic left-to-right shunts (e.g., VSD)** → long-term vascular remodeling and RV strain.  
- **Chronic thromboembolic disease** → progressive pulmonary vascular obstruction.  

Typical findings:
- **Dyspnea, fatigue, syncope** (from reduced cardiac output).  
- **Prominent right heart border** and **enlarged pulmonary arteries** on chest radiograph.  
- **Right-axis deviation or RV hypertrophy** on ECG.  
- **Tricuspid regurgitation and RV dilation** on echocardiogram.

---

### **6) Clinical Summary Table**

| **Category** | **Newborn (PPHN)** | **Adolescent / Chronic PH** |
|---------------|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Primary cause** | Failure of transitional circulation | Secondary to chronic disease or shunt |
| **O₂ response** | Improves with O₂ (reactive bed) | Often limited |
| **CXR findings** | Clear lungs, ↓ vascular markings | Prominent pulmonary arteries, cardiomegaly |
| **Echo** | Normal heart, R→L shunt, RV strain | RV hypertrophy, pulmonary artery dilation |
| **Main complication** | Hypoxia, hypotension | RV failure, arrhythmia |

---

### **7) Key Learning Points**
- PPHN should be suspected in **cyanotic neonates with clear lungs** and evidence of **R→L shunting**.  
- The **pre/post-ductal O₂ saturation difference >5–10%** is diagnostic and improves with oxygen in **reactive** PH.  
- **RV strain and tricuspid regurgitation** are cardinal echocardiographic features.  
- Older patients show evidence of **chronic RV remodeling** and **enlarged pulmonary arteries**.  
- Early recognition and treatment (oxygenation, nitric oxide, mechanical support) prevent progression to irreversible vascular remodeling.

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** PPHN presents with *minimal* lung disease, not diffuse parenchymal involvement.  
- **C:** RV strain and TR are hallmark findings, not absent.  
- **D:** Intracardiac shunts are *pathognomonic*, not exclusionary.  
- **E:** Hypotension results from *low LV preload*, not increased preload.

**Correct answer: B — Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn presents with severe hypoxia, differential pre/post-ductal saturations, and minimal radiographic lung findings.**
