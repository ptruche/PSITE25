A 26-year-old pregnant woman presents for counseling after an ultrasound reveals severe oligohydramnios and a large left-sided congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH). The maternal-fetal medicine specialist explains that several prenatal interventions are designed to improve lung development and reduce the risk of neonatal pulmonary hypertension. Which of the following statements best summarizes current principles of prevention?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

**Pulmonary hypertension (PH)** of the newborn often reflects disrupted lung and vascular development that begins **in utero**. Preventive strategies therefore focus on **optimizing fetal lung growth**, **reducing fetal and neonatal injury**, and **avoiding perinatal triggers** that elevate pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR). A combination of **prenatal**, **perinatal**, and **postnatal** measures can decrease both the incidence and severity of PH.

---

### **1) Prenatal Prevention — Enhancing Lung Growth and Development**
Pulmonary hypoplasia is one of the strongest structural contributors to neonatal PH. Preventive strategies aim to **improve lung parenchymal and vascular development** before birth:

| **Prenatal Intervention** | **Rationale / Mechanism** | **Example Conditions** |
|----------------------------|---------------------------|------------------------|
| **Treatment of oligohydramnios** | Restores amniotic fluid volume → reduces extrinsic lung compression | Amnioinfusion for prolonged rupture of membranes |
| **Fetal tracheal occlusion (FETO)** | Temporarily traps lung fluid → expands airways → stimulates lung growth | Severe congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH) |
| **Decompression of thoracic masses** | Reduces space-occupying compression on developing lungs | Thoracoamniotic shunt for macrocystic congenital lung lesion |
| **Avoidance of teratogenic drugs** | Prevents premature ductal closure and pulmonary vascular remodeling | Avoid NSAIDs and SSRIs late in pregnancy |
| **Control of maternal hypertension / hypoxia** | Improves uteroplacental perfusion | Pregnancy-induced hypertension, placental insufficiency |

By addressing modifiable maternal and fetal conditions, these measures promote **normal pulmonary vascular growth** and decrease fixed postnatal resistance.

---

### **2) Prevention of Infectious and Inflammatory Triggers**
**Vertically transmitted infections** and **neonatal sepsis** are important preventable causes of pulmonary hypertension. Inflammatory mediators from infection cause **endothelial injury, vasoconstriction, and surfactant dysfunction**. Strategies include:
- **Maternal infection screening and treatment** (e.g., group B Streptococcus prophylaxis, TORCH screening).  
- **Avoidance of premature rupture of membranes** and **prompt treatment of chorioamnionitis**.  
- **Aseptic perinatal care** and early antibiotic therapy in high-risk neonates.  

Preventing perinatal infection reduces pulmonary vascular inflammation and the risk of **persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN)**.

---

### **3) Prevention of Meconium Aspiration and Postnatal Injury**
**Meconium aspiration syndrome (MAS)** is a common postnatal cause of PH due to mechanical obstruction, inflammation, and **surfactant inactivation**. Prevention depends on **avoiding aspiration before birth or immediately at delivery**, not after symptoms develop.

| **Preventive Step** | **Mechanism** | **Effect** |
|----------------------|---------------|-------------|
| **Peripartum suctioning of the oropharynx/nasopharynx** | Removes meconium before first breaths | Prevents distal airway obstruction |
| **Avoid vigorous stimulation before airway clearance** | Prevents aspiration into lower airways | ↓ Surfactant inactivation, ↓ inflammation |
| **Prompt respiratory support after delivery** | Prevents hypoxia and hypercarbia | ↓ PVR |

Once meconium reaches distal airways, it **inactivates surfactant** and triggers an inflammatory cascade that elevates PVR. Thus, early suctioning and airway management are essential preventive measures.

---

### **4) Integrated Concept — From Fetus to Newborn**
Pulmonary hypertension prevention spans the **continuum of fetal to postnatal life**:

- **Prenatal:** Address lung compression, vascular maldevelopment, and maternal factors.  
- **Perinatal:** Prevent infection and aspiration.  
- **Postnatal:** Support oxygenation, ventilation, and vascular adaptation.  

| **Domain** | **Key Actions** | **Goal** |
|-------------|----------------|----------|
| **Prenatal** | Treat oligohydramnios, avoid teratogens, consider tracheal occlusion | Promote lung growth |
| **Perinatal** | Prevent infection, control labor stress, clear meconium | Minimize inflammatory injury |
| **Postnatal** | Optimize ventilation, oxygenation, and surfactant therapy | Support normal transition |

Together, these interventions reduce both **structural (fixed)** and **functional (reactive)** components of neonatal PH.

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** NSAIDs and SSRIs are *risk factors*, not protective.  
- **B:** Meconium prevention must occur *before* aspiration, not after distress develops.  
- **D:** Fetal tracheal occlusion improves—not worsens—lung development when carefully timed.  
- **E:** Preventive efforts extend beyond postnatal care to include maternal–fetal management.

**Correct answer: C — Optimizing fetal lung growth, preventing meconium aspiration, and reducing perinatal infection risk are key strategies to prevent pulmonary hypertension.**
