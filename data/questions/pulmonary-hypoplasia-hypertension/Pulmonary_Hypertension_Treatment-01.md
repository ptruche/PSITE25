A neonate with severe pulmonary hypertension of the newborn is receiving mechanical ventilation and inhaled nitric oxide but remains hypoxemic. The ICU team discusses adjustments to optimize management. Which of the following principles best reflects evidence-based medical therapy for pulmonary hypertension?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

**Management of pulmonary hypertension (PH)** requires a **multifaceted physiologic approach** to improve oxygen delivery, enhance cardiac performance, and reduce pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) — all while **avoiding iatrogenic injury**. The therapeutic strategy integrates **respiratory support, hemodynamic optimization, pulmonary vasodilation, and extracorporeal rescue**.

---

### **1) Goals of Therapy**
1. **Improve oxygenation and ventilation** to reverse hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction.  
2. **Support the right ventricle (RV)** against elevated afterload.  
3. **Reduce PVR** with pharmacologic and physiologic interventions.  
4. **Preserve systemic perfusion and end-organ oxygen delivery.**  

The foundation of therapy is to restore the balance between pulmonary and systemic circulation while protecting the lungs and myocardium from secondary injury.

---

### **2) Respiratory Management — Optimize Lung Recruitment, Avoid Injury**
**Ventilatory support** is critical but must be **gentle and targeted** to maintain **functional residual capacity (FRC)** without overdistention or collapse.

| **Strategy** | **Goal / Rationale** |
|---------------|----------------------|
| **Supplemental oxygen** | Pulmonary vasodilator; corrects hypoxemia and reverses vasoconstriction |
| **Optimal PEEP (not excessive)** | Prevents alveolar collapse but avoids capillary compression |
| **Avoid hyperventilation-induced alkalosis** | Previously used but now avoided due to lung injury (volu-/barotrauma) |
| **Target normocapnia / mild hypocapnia** | Prevents acidosis-induced vasoconstriction |
| **Maintain normal temperature, glucose, electrolytes** | Prevent metabolic contributors to vasoconstriction |

Both **atelectasis** and **overdistention** increase PVR. Thus, lung-protective ventilation strategies (e.g., moderate PEEP, permissive hypercapnia) are preferred.

---

### **3) Cardiac Support — Optimize Right Ventricular Performance**
The **right ventricle** in pulmonary hypertension faces high afterload and may fail without adequate support.  
**Inotropic support** helps maintain cardiac output and pulmonary blood flow.

| **Agent** | **Effect** | **Caution** |
|------------|------------|-------------|
| **Milrinone / prostanoids** | Pulmonary vasodilation; improves RV output | May cause systemic hypotension |
| **Epinephrine, dopamine (high dose)** | Increase contractility and cardiac output | Risk of pulmonary vasoconstriction |
| **Phenylephrine, norepinephrine** | Maintain systemic perfusion pressure | Excess dosing may worsen PVR |

Inotropes are titrated carefully to **enhance RV function** while avoiding excessive systemic vasoconstriction that increases pulmonary load.

---

### **4) Pulmonary Vasodilation — Selective and Targeted**
Specific **pulmonary vasodilators** directly reduce PVR without systemic hypotension:
- **Inhaled nitric oxide (iNO):** First-line therapy; increases cGMP, relaxing vascular smooth muscle.  
- **Sildenafil (PDE5 inhibitor):** Maintains cGMP; used in chronic or refractory cases.  
- **Prostacyclin analogs (epoprostenol, iloprost):** Increase cAMP-mediated vasodilation.  
- **Bosentan (endothelin receptor antagonist):** Long-term management in older patients with chronic PH.

Vasodilators should **not** be used indiscriminately — if LV dysfunction or pulmonary edema is present, these agents can **worsen oxygenation** by increasing shunt and pulmonary congestion.

---

### **5) Extracorporeal Life Support (ECLS)**
**ECLS (ECMO)** serves as a **salvage therapy** for severe, refractory PH unresponsive to maximal medical management.  
It allows:
- Gas exchange support while the lungs recover.  
- Reduction in ventilator pressures and oxygen toxicity.  
- Restoration of systemic oxygen delivery.

ECLS is **not initiated at diagnosis**, but rather when conventional ventilation, vasodilators, and inotropes fail to maintain oxygenation and perfusion.

---

### **6) Nonpharmacologic Optimization**
- Maintain **normothermia**, **euglycemia**, **electrolyte balance**, and **euvolemia**.  
- Prevent acidosis, hypothermia, and hypoglycemia — all potent triggers for pulmonary vasoconstriction.  
- Correct anemia to improve oxygen delivery.

---

### **7) Summary Table**

| **Therapeutic Target** | **Example** | **Rationale** |
|------------------------|-------------|----------------|
| **Oxygenation** | Oxygen, ventilator optimization | ↓ Hypoxic vasoconstriction |
| **Pulmonary vasodilation** | iNO, sildenafil, prostanoids | ↓ PVR |
| **RV function** | Inotropes, careful preload optimization | ↑ Cardiac output |
| **Systemic perfusion** | Maintain MAP with norepinephrine if needed | Avoid shock / organ injury |
| **Refractory hypoxemia** | ECMO | Rescue therapy |

---

**Key Points:**  
- **Avoid hyperventilation and excessive pressure ventilation**; both increase PVR.  
- **Optimize lung recruitment at FRC**, not maximal inflation.  
- **Use inotropes judiciously**; maintain systemic pressure without worsening PVR.  
- **ECLS** is reserved for severe, refractory cases after medical optimization.

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** Hyperventilation-induced alkalosis is outdated and harmful.  
- **B:** Overdistention increases, not decreases, PVR.  
- **D:** Aggressive systemic vasoconstriction may worsen RV workload.  
- **E:** ECLS is reserved for refractory cases, not first-line.  

**Correct answer: C — Optimal management of pulmonary hypertension focuses on improving oxygenation, optimizing right ventricular function, and minimizing pulmonary vascular resistance while avoiding ventilator-induced injury.**
