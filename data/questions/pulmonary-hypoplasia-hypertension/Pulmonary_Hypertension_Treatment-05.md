A 10-year-old child with chronic pulmonary hypertension refractory to sildenafil and nitric oxide is started on intravenous epoprostenol. Which of the following statements best describes the mechanism of action and clinical properties of prostanoid therapy?

<!-- EXPLANATION -->

**Prostanoids** are **synthetic analogues of prostacyclin (PGI₂)** that induce **pulmonary vasodilation** through activation of the **adenylate cyclase–cAMP signaling pathway**. They represent a cornerstone therapy in both acute and chronic pulmonary hypertension, particularly when other vasodilators fail.

---

### **1) Mechanism of Action**
- Prostacyclin binds to the **IP receptor (prostacyclin receptor)** on vascular smooth muscle.  
- This activates **adenylate cyclase**, converting **ATP → cyclic AMP (cAMP)**.  
- Increased **cAMP** activates **protein kinase A (PKA)**, leading to reduced intracellular calcium, smooth muscle relaxation, and **vasodilation**.  
- The overall effect is a **decrease in pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR)** and mild reduction in systemic blood pressure.

| **Pathway** | **Key Enzyme** | **Second Messenger** | **Effect** |
|--------------|----------------|----------------------|-------------|
| Prostacyclin (PGI₂) | Adenylate cyclase | ↑ cAMP | Pulmonary vasodilation |
| Epoprostenol / Iloprost | PDE3 inhibition (indirectly) | Sustains cAMP | Vasodilation, ↑ Cardiac output |

---

### **2) Epoprostenol (Sodium Prostacyclin)**
- **Epoprostenol** is a **short-acting, photolabile** prostanoid administered as a **continuous intravenous infusion**.  
- It decreases **PVR** while only **mildly reducing systemic blood pressure**.  
- At higher doses, it can **increase cardiac output** due to **positive inotropic effects** on the right ventricle.  
- Chronic IV epoprostenol therapy can **delay or eliminate the need for heart–lung transplantation** in severe, refractory pulmonary hypertension.

| **Feature** | **Description** |
|--------------|----------------|
| **Administration** | Continuous IV (via central line) |
| **Half-life** | 3–6 minutes (short) |
| **Light sensitivity** | Photolabile — requires light-protected tubing |
| **Effect** | ↓ PVR, mild ↓ systemic BP, ↑ CO |
| **Use** | Severe / refractory PH, bridge to transplant |

---

### **3) Inhaled Prostanoids (Iloprost)**
- **Iloprost** is an inhaled PGI₂ analogue with a longer half-life (20–30 min).  
- Used in **mechanically ventilated or ambulatory patients**, though frequent dosing (**≈6 inhalations per day**) is required.  
- In adults, iloprost reduces **mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP)** by ~5 mmHg and improves **exercise tolerance**.  
- **Pediatric data:** ~65% of children show improvement or stabilization; some discontinue due to **bronchoconstriction or airway reactivity**.  
- Use in mechanically ventilated patients may damage ventilator valves due to deposition.

| **Feature** | **Iloprost (Inhaled PGI₂ Analogue)** |
|--------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Mechanism** | ↑ cAMP → Vasodilation |
| **Dosing** | Inhaled 6×/day |
| **Advantages** | Selective pulmonary vasodilation |
| **Limitations** | Bronchoconstriction, ventilator valve damage, frequent dosing |

---

### **4) Other Prostanoid Formulations**
| **Agent** | **Route** | **Key Features** |
|------------|-----------|------------------|
| **Treprostinil** | Subcutaneous / IV | Longer half-life; less light-sensitive |
| **Beraprost** | Oral | Convenience, but limited efficacy |
| **Iloprost** | Inhaled | Intermittent dosing; airway irritation possible |

These agents allow individualized therapy depending on **disease severity**, **patient tolerance**, and **logistical considerations** (e.g., IV access vs inhalation device availability).

---

### **5) Clinical Effects**
- ↓ Pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR)  
- ↑ Right ventricular function and cardiac output  
- ↓ Mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP)  
- Mild ↓ systemic blood pressure  
- Improved **exercise tolerance** and **oxygenation**  

Chronic prostanoid therapy is particularly beneficial in **advanced pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH)**, where it can improve long-term survival.

---

### **6) Limitations and Adverse Effects**
- **Technical challenges:** continuous infusion, catheter infections, and pump malfunctions.  
- **Adverse effects:** headache, flushing, jaw pain, hypotension, and airway reactivity (especially with iloprost).  
- **Inhaled forms:** may cause bronchospasm; avoid in reactive airway disease.

---

### **7) Summary Table**

| **Agent** | **Mechanism** | **Route** | **Advantages** | **Limitations** |
|------------|---------------|-----------|----------------|----------------|
| **Epoprostenol** | ↑ cAMP via adenylate cyclase | IV | Potent, improves survival | Requires central line, photolabile |
| **Iloprost** | ↑ cAMP | Inhaled | Pulmonary-selective | Bronchospasm, frequent dosing |
| **Treprostinil** | ↑ cAMP | SC / IV | Longer half-life | Site pain (SC), systemic effects |
| **Beraprost** | ↑ cAMP | Oral | Convenience | Limited pediatric data |

---

**Incorrect answers:**  
- **A:** Prostanoids act on cAMP, not cGMP.  
- **B:** Iloprost requires multiple daily inhalations and can cause airway reactivity.  
- **D:** Epoprostenol *decreases* systemic BP and PVR; does not raise BP.  
- **E:** Chronic prostanoid therapy improves survival and may delay transplant.  

**Correct answer: C — Prostanoids such as epoprostenol and iloprost act via adenylate cyclase to increase cAMP, promoting vasodilation and improved right ventricular output.**
