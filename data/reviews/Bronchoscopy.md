# Bronchoscopy 
---

## <span style="color:#4B0082;">Overview</span>

Bronchoscopy is an essential diagnostic and therapeutic procedure in pediatric surgery.  
It allows **direct visualization of the airway** for diagnosis and treatment of:

- Foreign body aspiration  
- Congenital airway anomalies (TEF, tracheomalacia, laryngeal clefts)  
- Airway trauma and stenosis  
- Inhalation injury  
- Endobronchial tumors  

Pediatric airway management demands an understanding of **unique anatomic and physiologic challenges** and mastery of **rigid bronchoscopy**, the gold standard for pediatric airway intervention.

---

## <span style="color:#1E90FF;">Anatomy & Airway Challenges</span>

The pediatric airway is **narrow, anterior, and reactive**.  

| **Feature** | **Pediatric Characteristic** | **Clinical Implication** |
|--------------|-----------------------------|---------------------------|
| Occiput | Prominent | Causes neck flexion when supine → use shoulder roll |
| Tongue | Relatively large | Can obstruct airway → jaw thrust important |
| Epiglottis | Floppy, U-shaped | Lifts poorly → Miller or anterior commissure scope preferred |
| Larynx | More anterior (C3–C4) | Visualization more difficult |
| Trachea | Narrow, compliant | High risk of collapse and obstruction |

**Three-Axis Alignment Theory:** Align oral, pharyngeal, and laryngeal axes by flexing neck and extending head.

<span style="color:#1E90FF;font-weight:bold">Pearl:</span> Always evaluate for syndromic predictors of a difficult airway — Pierre Robin, Treacher Collins, trisomy 21, mucopolysaccharidosis.

---

## <span style="color:#006400;">Indications</span>

| **Category** | **Examples** |
|---------------|--------------|
| **Congenital** | Tracheoesophageal fistula, laryngotracheoesophageal cleft, tracheomalacia, vascular compression |
| **Acquired** | Airway foreign body, trauma, inhalation injury, stenosis, endobronchial mass |
| **Diagnostic** | Persistent wheeze, recurrent pneumonia, chronic cough |
| **Therapeutic** | Foreign body removal, lavage, stent placement, biopsy, laser ablation |

<span style="color:#1E90FF;font-weight:bold">Exam Fact:</span> Rigid bronchoscopy remains the **gold standard** for airway foreign body removal in children.

---

## <span style="color:#1E90FF;">Contraindications</span>
- **Absolute:** inability to ventilate or oxygenate, unstable airway not amenable to intervention.  
- **Relative:** coagulopathy, cervical spine instability, severe hypoxia (stabilize first).

---

## <span style="color:#006400;">Preoperative Preparation</span>

- Review and test all scopes, optics, light sources, suction, and instruments.  
- Discuss procedural plan and ventilation strategy with anesthesia.  
- Position supine with **shoulder roll**; extend head unless cervical injury suspected.  
- Anticipate **rapid desaturation** due to high O₂ consumption and small FRC.  
- Have **emergency airway setup**: 14–12 g angiocatheter with 3.0 ETT hub, tracheostomy set.

## <span style="color:#4B0082;">Technique: Rigid Bronchoscopy</span>

1. Confirm equipment and anesthesia plan.  
2. Apply topical lidocaine to cords to minimize spasm.  
3. Visualize cords using Miller or anterior commissure scope.  
4. Advance bronchoscope through glottis under vision.  
5. Evaluate **distal bronchi first**, then withdraw proximally.  
6. Coordinate ventilation with anesthesia (periods of apnea ↔ ventilation).  
7. Retrieve object or perform intervention; re-inspect both bronchi.  
8. Obtain post-procedure chest X-ray.

<span style="color:#006400;font-weight:bold">Technique Tip:</span> Keep the foreign body **inside the scope barrel** during withdrawal to prevent dislodgment.

---

## <span style="color:#4B0082;">Technique: Flexible Bronchoscopy</span>

- May be performed awake (with topical lidocaine) or under sedation/general anesthesia.  
- Route: nasal, oral (bite block), via ETT, LMA, or tracheostomy.  
- Allows dynamic airway visualization (malacia), BAL, and selective suctioning.  
- Retrieval tools: baskets, snares, nets, graspers, or Fogarty catheter alongside the scope.

---

## <span style="color:#1E90FF;">Flexible vs Rigid Bronchoscopy</span>

| **Feature** | **Rigid** | **Flexible** |
|--------------|-----------|--------------|
| Airway control | Excellent | Limited |
| Visualization | Oropharynx → lobar bronchi | Segmental/distal bronchi |
| Working channel | Large (≥ 4 mm) | Small (≤ 2 mm) |
| Ventilation | Controlled via side port | Spontaneous/assisted |
| Indications | Foreign body, bleeding, surgical airway | Dynamic airway, malacia, distal pathology |
| Setting | OR | ICU, endoscopy suite |

---

## <span style="color:#006400;">Foreign Body Aspiration</span>

**Epidemiology:**  
- Leading cause of accidental death in infants; 80 % occur < 3 years.  
- Organic objects (peanuts, seeds) predominate.  

**Pathophysiology:**  
- *Ball-valve effect* → air trapping → unilateral hyperinflation.  
- Most lodge in the **right mainstem bronchus**.

**Imaging Findings:**  
- Unilateral hyperinflation, mediastinal shift, air trapping on decubitus film.  
- Radiopaque object on CXR (rare).  
- CT chest → 100 % concordance with bronchoscopy when diagnosis uncertain.

<span style="color:#1E90FF;font-weight:bold">Pearl:</span> Normal CXR + credible aspiration history = bronchoscopy indicated.

---

### **Procedure & Adjuncts**
- Perform under general anesthesia with spontaneous or controlled ventilation.  
- Avoid positive pressure pre-removal to prevent distal migration.  
- **Fogarty balloon catheter**: slide past object, inflate, and retract.  
- **Laser or APC**: release chronically embedded object.  
- **Thoracotomy** only if bronchoscopic removal fails.

---

### **Post-Removal Management**
- Re-inspect both bronchi; obtain post-procedure CXR.  
- Treat wheezing with inhaled/systemic steroids.  
- Repeat bronchoscopy if granulation or residual debris.

<span style="color:#1E90FF;font-weight:bold">Exam Fact:</span> Bleeding < 3 %; manage with aerosolized epinephrine or tamponade.

---

## <span style="color:#4B0082;">Bronchoscopy in TEF and Esophageal Atresia</span>

- Identifies **fistula location**, associated **laryngeal cleft**, and **tracheomalacia**.  
- Intraoperative balloon or ETT tip can **occlude distal TEF**.  
- Post-repair evaluation for recurrent TEF:  
  - Instill methylene blue into trachea; observe in esophagus.  
  - Probe with catheter through bronchoscope.  
- Malacia visualized as *anterior-posterior collapse* (“fishmouthing”).  
- BAL revealing lipid-laden macrophages → chronic aspiration.

---

## <span style="color:#4B0082;">Airway Trauma</span>

**Suspect when:** continuous air leak or persistent pneumothorax after chest tube.  
**Evaluate:** flexible bronchoscopy to locate tear; intubate contralateral bronchus if needed.  
**Repair:** thoracotomy for tracheobronchial junction injury.  
**Complications:** missed injury → collapse, bronchiectasis, stenosis.

---

## <span style="color:#4B0082;">Inhalation Injury</span>

**Indications for Early Intubation:** facial burns, soot, singed nasal hairs, hoarseness, stridor.  
**Bronchoscopy:** assesses mucosal injury, debris, edema; guides extubation timing.  
**Labs:** carboxyhemoglobin, cyanide levels.  
**Treatment:** 100 % O₂, hydroxocobalamin for cyanide toxicity.

---

## <span style="color:#4B0082;">Dynamic Airway Lesions</span>

| **Condition** | **Bronchoscopic Finding** | **Management** |
|----------------|---------------------------|----------------|
| Tracheomalacia | > 50 % collapse of walls during expiration | Observation → CPAP → Aortopexy/Tracheopexy |
| Bronchomalacia | Segmental collapse distal to main bronchi | Similar algorithm |
| Airway Stenosis | Fixed narrowing | Balloon dilation, laser, resection |

---

## <span style="color:#B22222;">Complications & Management</span>

| **Complication** | **Mechanism** | **Treatment** |
|------------------|---------------|----------------|
| Hypoxia / hypercarbia | Airway obstruction, prolonged apnea | Remove scope, ventilate |
| Laryngospasm / bronchospasm | Irritation | Deepen anesthesia, β-agonists |
| Bleeding | Trauma, biopsy | Tamponade, topical epi (1:10,000), APC |
| Pneumothorax | Airway injury | Chest tube |
| Airway perforation | Rigid scope trauma | Surgical repair |
| Stridor / edema | Mucosal trauma | Racemic epi, steroids |
| Dental injury | Instrument insertion | Protective bite guard |

<span style="color:#B22222;font-weight:bold">Pitfall:</span> Cricothyrotomy is contraindicated < 8 years — use transtracheal angiocatheter instead.

---

## <span style="color:#1E90FF;">Postoperative Care & Follow-Up</span>

- Post-bronchoscopy CXR to rule out pneumothorax and residual object.  
- ICU monitoring if airway edema or multiple retrieval attempts.  
- Repeat bronchoscopy for residual fragments or granulation.  
- Long-term: surveillance for recurrence (carcinoid), recurrent TEF, or airway collapse.

---

## <span style="color:#1E90FF;">Evaluation of Stridor</span>

| **Type** | **Location** | **Common Etiologies** | **Bronchoscopic Approach** |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------------|-----------------------------|
| Inspiratory | Nasopharynx / oropharynx | Choanal atresia, macroglossia, vallecular cyst | Laryngoscopy / oropharyngeal |
| Expiratory | Intrathoracic trachea | Foreign body, asthma | Rigid bronchoscopy |
| Biphasic | Larynx / cervical trachea | Laryngomalacia, subglottic stenosis, web, hemangioma | Nasal or oral bronchoscopy |

---

## <span style="color:#4B0082;">PSITE-Level High-Yield Summary</span>

<span style="color:#1E90FF;font-weight:bold">PEARLS</span>
- History > Imaging for aspiration suspicion.  
- Normal CXR does not exclude foreign body.  
- Decubitus or expiratory films show air trapping.  
- Right mainstem > left for foreign body lodging.  
- Rigid bronchoscopy = therapeutic standard.  
- Methylene blue test confirms recurrent TEF.  
- Fogarty catheter aids distal retrieval.  
- Three-axis alignment for optimal exposure.  
- Infants desaturate quickly → coordinate anesthesia.  
- Always obtain post-procedure CXR.  
- Bleeding control = Tamponade + Epi + APC.

<span style="color:#B22222;font-weight:bold">COMMON TEST QUESTIONS</span>
1. Preferred bronchoscopy type for foreign body? → Rigid.  
2. Management of desaturation during bronchoscopy? → Remove scope lens, ventilate, reoxygenate.  
3. What test confirms recurrent TEF? → Methylene blue.  
4. What age to avoid cricothyrotomy? → Under 8 years.  
5. Post-removal step? → Re-inspect both bronchi + CXR.
