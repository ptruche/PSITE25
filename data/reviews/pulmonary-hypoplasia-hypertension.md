# Pulmonary Hypertension & Pulmonary Hypoplasia

---

## <span style="color:#006400;">1) Epidemiology</span>

### Pulmonary Hypertension (PHTN)
- **True incidence:** not precisely defined; ongoing registries (PPHNet).
- **PPHN (U.S. NICU):** **0.43–6.82 per 1000** liveborn neonates (**≈1.9/1000** average).
- **Risk factors**  
  **Prenatal:** **CDH**, **congenital heart disease**, **oligohydramnios**, maternal exposure (**ASA/NSAIDs**, **SSRIs**), **peripartum infections**, **fetal hypoxia**.  
  **Postnatal:** **prematurity**, **meconium aspiration**, **neonatal sepsis**, **polycythemia**, **perinatal asphyxia**, **congenital lung lesions**/mass effect, **omphalocele**, **surfactant deficiency (RDS)**, **BPD**, **idiopathic**.

### Pulmonary Hypoplasia
- **Overall incidence (liveborn):** **9–10 per 10,000**.  
- **Neonatal autopsies:** present in **up to 22%**.  
- **Associated conditions/populations at highest risk:**  
  **CDH** (~**2.4/10,000**), **CPAM** (unknown population incidence), **omphalocele**, **severe oligohydramnios**, **posterior urethral valves/lower urinary tract obstruction** (~**2.2/10,000**), **renal agenesis**, others.

<span style="color:#1E90FF;font-weight:bold">Pearl:</span> Many prenatal contributors to hypoplasia also elevate **postnatal PVR**, linking hypoplasia to PHTN pathophysiology.

---

## <span style="color:#4B0082;">2) Presentation</span>

### Pulmonary Hypertension — Physical Findings
- **Tachypnea** (earliest sign) and **increased work of breathing**.
- **Central cyanosis** is ominous; may see differential saturations (pre-/post-ductal).
- Hypoxemia, hypercarbia, systemic hypotension if LV preload impaired.

### Pulmonary Hypoplasia — Physical Findings
- **Tachypnea**, **WOB**, neonatal respiratory distress **mirroring PHTN** features.
- In severe (“lethal”) hypoplasia: **inability to normalize PaCO₂** despite maximal ventilation.

---

## <span style="color:#1E90FF;">3) Diagnosis</span>

### Pulmonary Hypertension — Laboratory & Imaging Criteria
- **Hemodynamic definition:** **mPAP ≥ 25 mm Hg** at rest (cath).  
  Pragmatic bedside marker: **right-sided pressures > ~2/3 systemic**.
- **ABG:** **hypoxemia ± hypercarbia**; track **A-a gradient** and **Oxygenation Index (OI)**.  
  • **OI = (FiO₂ × MAP × 100) / PaO₂**  
- **Severe gas exchange impairment:** **OI > 40** or **A-a > 620**.  
- **CXR:** confirm ETT position; assess **overinflation**, exclude **pneumothorax**; may show **decreased vascular markings** with intracardiac shunt; identify secondary lung disease (pneumonia/aspiration).  
- **Echocardiogram:** elevated right-sided pressures; **flattened/left-bowed septum**; **R→L or bidirectional PDA/PFO shunt**; **TR jet**; **RV-RA gradient > 30 mm Hg**; potential **LV underfilling**.

### Pulmonary Hypoplasia — Diagnostic Features
- **ABG:** **hypoxemia + hypercarbia**; **lethal hypoplasia** → persistent hypercarbia **despite** maximal support.  
- **CXR:** **small lungs** (uni-/bilateral) ± mass effect (**CDH**, **CPAM**).  
- **Echo:** suggests **fixed/high PVR** via reduced capillary bed (decreased total cross-sectional area), often non-reversible early.

<span style="color:#1E90FF;font-weight:bold">Exam Fact:</span> **Hypoplasia + PHTN** often coexist; distinguish **recruitable lung** failure from **fixed vascular bed** limitation.

---

## <span style="color:#006400;">4) Pertinent Physiology</span>

### Fetal & Transitional Circulation (Normal)
- **In utero:** pulmonary bed = **high-resistance parallel circuit**; only **<10–15%** of RV output traverses lungs; remainder bypasses via **PFO/PDA**.
- **Transition:** first breaths → alveolar fluid clearance + oxygenation → **vasodilation**; **PFO** functionally closes; **ductus** constricts.
- **Normalization:** **PVR falls rapidly** but typically **normalizes by 4–6 weeks** of life.

### Abnormal Pulmonary Physiology in PHTN
- **Hyperreactive vasoconstriction**, **excess distal muscularization**, and **reduced vascular bed (hypoplasia)** raise **PVR**.
- **Volume dependence:** both **overdistension** (high PEEP) and **underinflation** raise PVR → target **FRC**.

### Normal Fetal Lung Development Stages
| Stage | Timing | Key Events |
|---|---|---|
| **Embryonic** | 0–6 wks | Lung bud forms |
| **Pseudoglandular** | 7–16 wks | Branching of first ~16–25 airway generations |
| **Canalicular** | 16–24 wks | Final generations of branching; canaliculi form |
| **Saccular** | 24–40 wks | Interstitium thins; saccules expand |
| **Alveolar** | Birth–2 yrs (→ ~8 yrs) | ~20M sacs at birth → **~300M alveoli** develop through childhood |

<span style="color:#1E90FF;font-weight:bold">Pearl:</span> Impaired development in the **canalicular–saccular** phases underlies many cases of **hypoplasia** and **fixed PVR**.

---

## <span style="color:#4B0082;">5) Staging / Grading Severity</span>

### Pulmonary Hypertension — Quantitative & Qualitative
**Quantitative (examples):**
- **TR jet > 40 mm Hg** → elevated right-sided pressures.
- **OI > 40** or **A-a > 620** → life-threatening gas exchange impairment.

**Qualitative echo features:**
- **Flattened / LV-bowed septum** (systolic)
- **Dilated RV** (>**60–70%** of LV cross-sectional area)
- **PDA shunt directionality** across the cardiac cycle (R→L or bidirectional)

### Pulmonary Hypoplasia — Imaging Severity & Outcomes
- **Fetal US/MRI**:  
  • **Observed-to-expected Lung-to-Head Ratio (o/e LHR)**  
  • **Observed-to-expected Total Fetal Lung Volume (o/e TFLV)**  
- **Severity thresholds (o/e LHR or TFLV):**  
  **<25% = severe**, **26–45% = moderate**, **>45% = mild**  
  **Survival linkage (o/e TFLV):** ~**18%** (<25%), **66%** (26–45%), **89%** (>45%).  
- Postnatal severity also reflected by **OI**/**A-a**.

## <span style="color:#006400;">6) Nonsurgical Treatment (Initial & Endothelial-Targeted)</span>

### Initial Support (applies to PHTN ± Hypoplasia)
- **Supplemental oxygen** (avoid hyperoxia-related harm but correct hypoxemia)
- Normalize **intravascular volume**, **electrolytes**, **glucose**, **temperature**
- **Analgesia/sedation** to blunt catecholamine surges; avoid cold/nociceptive triggers
- **Mechanical ventilation**: aim to ventilate **around FRC** (avoid atelectrauma & overdistension)
- **Cardiac support**: **inotropy/vasopressors** to maintain RV/systemic perfusion

### Pulmonary Endothelium–Targeted Medical Therapy
| Class | Agent | Pathway | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inhaled vasodilator** | **iNO** (5–40 ppm) | **NO–sGC–cGMP↑** | Best in **MAS/RDS**; limited in **CDH**; methemoglobinemia/NO₂ risk; nonresponders at low ppm rarely benefit from higher levels |
| **PDE-5 inhibitor** | **Sildenafil** (PO/IV) | **cGMP↑** (degradation ↓) | CYP3A4 metabolism; caution with combo vasodilators (**hypotension**); pediatric black box context for chronic high dose |
| **PDE-3 inhibitor** | **Milrinone** (IV) | **cAMP↑** (lusitropy/afterload↓) | Helpful in LV diastolic dysfunction, augments iNO; titrate for **hypotension**; renal clearance |
| **Prostanoids** | **Epoprostenol (IV)**, **Iloprost (inh.)**, **Treprostinil (SC/IV)** | **PGI₂–cAMP↑** | Systemic vasodilation; inhaled iloprost—mixed pediatric data; practical burden |
| **ET-1 antagonist** | **Bosentan** (PO) | **Endothelin A/B blockade** | Improves oxygenation index in some series; **LFT monitoring** required |
| **Surfactant** | **Exogenous surfactant** | Improves compliance, V/Q | Especially in **RDS**, **MAS**; may reduce PVR indirectly by recruitment |

<span style="color:#B22222;font-weight:bold">Pitfall:</span> **LV dysfunction** + aggressive **iNO** can **worsen pulmonary edema** and hypoxemia. Prioritize **inotropy/afterload** and careful diuresis before escalating vasodilators.

---

## <span style="color:#4B0082;">7) ECLS (ECMO) — Indications & Context</span>

- **Role:** temporary **gas exchange and/or cardiac support** when mortality risk >~50% despite optimal conventional therapy.
- **Indications (contextual):** **PPHN**, **sepsis**, **RDS**, **PHTN**, **pulmonary hypoplasia (e.g., CDH)**.  
  • Best survival on ECLS: **meconium aspiration** and **sepsis**.  
  • **CDH on ECLS:** ~**51%** survival (recent reports may be higher in selected centers).
- **Modes:** **Venoarterial (VA)** and **Venovenous (VV)**.
- **Contraindications/controversy:** **“Lethal” pulmonary hypoplasia** is a **contraindication**, yet diagnosis may be uncertain at decision time; genetics/metabolic and severity assessments may require time.

---

## <span style="color:#1E90FF;">8) Assessment of Outcomes</span>

- Determinants: **underlying etiology**, **severity of PHTN**, **response to therapy**, **need/duration of ventilation**, **ECLS use**, and **associated anomalies**.
- **CDH-related hypoplasia:** contemporary overall survival **~75–80%** (from ~50% 25 years ago).  
  • The **sickest CDH infants** echo the morbidity profile of chronic disease survivors (feeding, pulmonary, neurodevelopment).
- **PPHN neurodevelopment:** adverse outcomes **~14–30%**; long-term oxygen/GT/home meds **not significantly different** between conventional vs iNO groups → suggests injury antecedent to therapy initiation.

---

## <span style="color:#006400;">9) Long-Term Follow-Up</span>

- **PPHN:** heightened risk of **neurodevelopmental impairment**; structured developmental screening and hearing/vision surveillance recommended.
- **Pulmonary hypoplasia (especially CDH):** follow **AAP CDH survivor guidelines**  
  — growth/feeding, pulmonary and cardiac function testing, developmental & neurodevelopmental screening (hearing, neuroimaging as indicated), and musculoskeletal evaluations.  
- Multidisciplinary clinics (surgery, neonatology, pulmonology, cardiology, nutrition, OT/PT/SLP) optimize longitudinal outcomes.

## <span style="color:#1E90FF;">Algorithmic Approach (Neonate with Suspected PHTN ± Hypoplasia)</span>

1. **Stabilize Airway & Ventilate at FRC** (avoid atelectasis/overdistension).  
2. **Correct hypoxemia, acidosis, hypothermia, electrolytes, glucose**.  
3. **Echo** to define shunts, RV pressure/strain, LV function.  
4. **Hemodynamics:** judicious fluids; inotropy/vasopressors to support RV/systemic flow.  
5. If LV function acceptable → **iNO**; add **milrinone** for lusitropy/afterload.  
6. If inadequate response → **sildenafil / prostanoids / bosentan** (context dependent).  
7. **LV dysfunction present?** Minimize vasodilators that worsen pulmonary edema; prioritize **inotropy/afterload** and diuresis.  
8. **Refractory hypoxemia/acidosis:** **ECLS** consideration.  
9. Evaluate for **CTEPH/CHD** contributors when appropriate (cath/CT/MRI).

---

## <span style="color:#B22222;">Common Pitfalls (PSITE Traps)</span>

- Interpreting a **normal CXR** as exclusion of severe PHTN/hypoplasia—clinical/echo context is crucial.  
- Escalating **iNO** despite **LV dysfunction** → worsening edema and oxygenation.  
- Ventilating **above/below FRC**: both raise PVR; titrate to recruitment without over-PEEP.  
- Assuming **vasodilators** benefit **fixed PVR** from profound hypoplasia—focus shifts to support, ECLS, or palliation in truly lethal forms.

## <span style="color:#4B0082;">Board-Style Review Prompts</span>

**1. Incidence & Risks:** State U.S. **PPHN** incidence and list **prenatal/postnatal** risk factors.  
**2. Diagnostic Thresholds:** Define **mPAP ≥25**, cite **TR jet >40**, **OI >40**, **A-a >620**, and echo markers (septal flattening, PDA shunt).  
**3. Hypoplasia Severity:** Interpret **o/e LHR**/**o/e TFLV** thresholds and linked survival.  
**4. Vent Strategy:** Explain why **FRC** targeting lowers PVR (vs underinflation/overdistension).  
**5. iNO Caveat:** Why can **iNO** worsen oxygenation in **LV dysfunction**?  
**6. ECLS Context:** Which conditions have best/worse ECLS survival (MAS/sepsis vs CDH)?  
**7. Outcomes:** Provide contemporary **CDH survival 75–80%** and **PPHN neurodevelopment 14–30%** ranges.

**Summary Statement:**  
Pediatric **PHTN** arises from **elevated PVR** due to **vasoreactivity, distal muscularization, and/or reduced vascular bed** (hypoplasia). High-fidelity care targets **FRC ventilation**, **RV-centric hemodynamics**, and **endothelial pathways** (NO–cGMP, PGI₂–cAMP, endothelin antagonism); **ECLS** bridges refractory cases. **Hypoplasia** severity is best anticipated prenatally (o/e LHR/TFLV) and guides realistic postnatal goals, with improving **CDH survival** yet meaningful long-term morbidity that demands structured, multidisciplinary follow-up.
